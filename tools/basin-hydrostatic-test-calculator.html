Water-tightness (liquid loss) testing of concrete basins, tanks, and channels per the ACI 350.1 method: corrected water surface drop against the allowable percentage of volume per 24 hours. Runs entirely offline; inputs persist in this browser via localStorage.

How the Numbers Are Derived

The math

Corrected leakage drop:

Pan method: Δh = drop_basin − Δh_pan (the pan sees the same evaporation and rainfall, so subtracting it isolates leakage).
Split method: Δh = drop_basin − evaporation + rainfall.

Volume lost: V_lost = Δh/12 × A × 7.48 gal. Loss rate normalized to 24 hr:

%/24hr = V_lost / V_test × (24 / t_hr) × 100

The pre-test planning number — the allowable drop in inches over your test duration:

Δh_allow = (allow%/100) × V_test / (A × 7.48) × 12 × (t_hr/24)

Running the test so the numbers mean something
  • Stabilization first. New or long-dry concrete absorbs water; ACI 350.1 requires the basin sit full for a stabilization period (commonly 3 days, per the standard/spec) before the measured test starts, or an early "failure" is just the concrete drinking.
  • Measurement precision governs duration. If the allowable drop over 24 hr is smaller than you can reliably measure (roughly 1/16 in. with a good hook gauge or fixed staff), extend the test — the standard allows longer durations for exactly this reason. The tool flags when the allowable drop is under 0.25 in.
  • Fixed reference points. Measure from set gauges or marked points at multiple locations, at the same time of day (thermal expansion of the water is real over a big surface).
  • Valves and bulkheads count as leaks. A weeping plug valve or temporary bulkhead fails the basin on paper. Isolate and verify them before blaming the concrete.
  • Damp spots vs. flowing water. Most specs distinguish acceptable dampness from unacceptable flowing/trickling leakage on the exterior regardless of the volumetric result — walk the walls, don't just read the gauge.
