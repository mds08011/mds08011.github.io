This tool implements the textbook/TR-55 forms; where the county hydrology manual differs (and in Southern California it usually does — several counties use their own Modified Rational procedures), the manual governs. How the Numbers Are Derived

The Rational Method

Q = C · i · A — Q in cfs, i in in/hr, A in acres. (The unit conversion factor is 1.008, universally taken as 1.0.) Composite C is the area-weighted average: ΣC·A / ΣA. The core assumption: peak flow occurs when the entire watershed contributes, i.e. when storm duration equals Tc — which is why intensity is read at Tc.

TR-55 travel time segments

Sheet flow (first ≤100 ft of the path): Tt = 0.007·(nL)^0.8 / (P₂^0.5 · s^0.4) hours — n is the sheet-flow roughness (smooth surfaces 0.011, short grass 0.15, dense grass 0.24, light woods 0.40), L in ft, s in ft/ft, P₂ the 2-yr 24-hr rainfall in inches. Current NRCS guidance limits sheet flow length to 100 ft; the tool warns beyond that.

Shallow concentrated flow: V = 20.328·√s (paved) or 16.135·√s (unpaved) ft/s, then Tt = L / (3600·V).

Channel flow: Tt = L / (3600·V) with V entered directly — compute it with the Manning's tool for the actual channel or pipe section.

Tc is the sum of segment travel times along the hydraulically longest path, floored at the entered minimum.

Typical C values (verify against the local table)

Roofs and pavement 0.85–0.95 · gravel 0.35–0.70 · lawns, flat sandy 0.05–0.10 · lawns, steep clayey 0.25–0.35 · undeveloped brush 0.20–0.40. Many manuals adjust C upward for rarer storms (e.g. ×1.1–1.25 for 25–100 yr) — apply that per the manual if required, it is not automatic here.

Field/reviewer notes
  • The single most common error in Rational Method submittals is a Tc path that isn't actually the hydraulically longest one — walking the site beats tracing the obvious swale on the plan.
  • An absurdly short Tc (all pipe, steep site) drives intensity — and Q — way up. That's the model working correctly; it's also why criteria floor Tc.
  • For pre- vs. post-development comparisons, keep the same method, storm, and IDF source on both sides or the comparison is meaningless.
  • Above the method's area limit, or with significant storage/attenuation in the watershed, switch to a hydrograph method (TR-55/TR-20, or the county's unit hydrograph).
