How the Numbers Are Derived

Linear scaling

% = (mA − 4) / 16 × 100

PV = LRV + (mA − 4)/16 × (URV − LRV)    and inverted:   mA = 4 + 16 × (PV − LRV)/(URV − LRV)

Works for elevated and suppressed zeros (LRV ≠ 0, including negative LRV for vacuum or bidirectional flow) — the span is just URV − LRV, signed.

Square-root (DP flow) scaling

Differential pressure across an orifice or venturi varies with the square of flow, so when the transmitter sends raw DP as a linear signal, flow is recovered as:

PV = LRV + √((mA − 4)/16) × (URV − LRV)

The consequences on the checkout table are the part that trips people: 50% signal (12 mA) is 70.7% of full flow, and 25% signal (8 mA) is 50% flow. If the SCADA readback during a loop check matches the linear column instead of the square-root column, the extraction is configured in the wrong place (or twice).

Fault bands (NAMUR NE 43)

Readings outside the live 4–20 range are diagnostic, not data:

  • 3.8 – 20.5 mA — valid measuring range including linear over/under-range margin.
  • ≤ 3.6 mA — transmitter signaling failure (downscale), or a wiring/power problem.
  • ≥ 21.0 mA — transmitter signaling failure (upscale).
  • 0 mA — open loop: broken wire, blown fuse, dead power supply, or the meter is in the wrong spot.

The tool flags any converted mA reading in these bands rather than pretending it's a process value.

Field notes
  • Measure current in series, or read the voltage across the loop's 250 Ω resistor and divide (12.00 mA ↔ 3.000 V) — no need to break a running loop.
  • Confirm LRV/URV from the transmitter's own display or HART, not from the instrument index — ranges get changed during startup and the paper lags.
  • Level transmitters mounted below the tank connection have an elevated-zero offset; that's what a negative or nonzero LRV is for.
  • If SCADA and the field reading disagree by a consistent scale factor, someone scaled 0–100% where engineering units were expected (or vice versa) at the PLC — the five-point table finds this in one pass.
