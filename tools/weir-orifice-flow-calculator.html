Use limits: These formulas assume a properly built and installed sharp-crested weir with a free (aerated) nappe, or a clean sharp-edged orifice, and head measured correctly. How the Numbers Are Derived

Weir formulas (US units: Q in cfs, h and L in ft)
  • 90° V-notch (USBR): Q = 2.49 · h^2.48
  • V-notch, other angles: Q = 2.5 · tan(θ/2) · h^2.5 (standard field form)
  • Rectangular, contracted (Francis): Q = 3.33 · (L − 0.2h) · h^1.5
  • Rectangular, suppressed: Q = 3.33 · L · h^1.5
  • Cipolletti: Q = 3.367 · L · h^1.5 (the 1:4 side slopes compensate for end contractions)
  • Submerged orifice: Q = C_d · A · √(2gΔh)

Conversions: 1 cfs = 448.83 GPM = 0.6463 MGD.

Conditions the formulas assume (the accuracy fine print)
  • Head range: most sharp-crested weir formulas are rated for roughly 0.2–2.0 ft of head. Below ~0.2 ft, surface tension and nappe clinging dominate and accuracy falls apart — the tool warns.
  • Free nappe: air must circulate under the falling sheet of water. A nappe that clings to the downstream face over-reads. Suppressed rectangular weirs need vents.
  • Approach conditions: low approach velocity (weir pool, not a flume), crest well above the downstream water surface (no submergence), and the crest at least ~2× the head above the upstream channel bottom.
  • Contracted weirs: the Francis end-contraction correction assumes the notch is well away from the channel sides (≥ 2h each side).
  • Sharp crest: the upstream edge should be a true sharp edge (¼-in. plate chamfered downstream, or angle iron) — a 2× lumber crest is a broad-crested weir with a different, mushier coefficient.
Choosing a device in the field
  • V-notch for low/variable flows (best sensitivity at low Q — a 90° notch reads roughly 0.9 to 450 GPM over 1–12 in. of head).
  • Rectangular or Cipolletti for higher flows where a V-notch would need excessive head.
  • Submerged orifice where there isn't enough drop for a weir to run free.
  • For anything that needs defensible accuracy over days (billing, permit reporting), rent a flume or a proper meter — a temporary weir is a startup verification device.
