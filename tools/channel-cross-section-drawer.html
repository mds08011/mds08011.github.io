How the Numbers Are Derived

Manning's equation and section geometry

V = (1.486/n) · R^(2/3) · √S and Q = V · A (US customary; the 1.486 is the unit factor, 1.0 in SI). Trapezoidal: A = (b + Zy)y, P = b + 2y√(1+Z²), T = b + 2Zy. Circular with central angle θ = 2·acos(1 − 2y/D): A = (D²/8)(θ − sinθ), P = Dθ/2, T = D·sin(θ/2).

Normal depth solver and the 94% quirk

Manning's can't be inverted for depth in closed form, so solving a design flow bisects Q(y) to the target. For a circular pipe, discharge peaks near d/D ≈ 0.94, not full — past that, added wetted perimeter costs more than the added area gains, so a "full" pipe carries slightly less than a 94%-full one. The drawing makes this visible: the shaded segment at max discharge doesn't reach the crown. A design flow above the peak is reported as exceeding gravity capacity.

Field notes
  • The 1.486 factor is the US-customary form of Manning's; use 1.0 with SI units. Everything here is US customary (feet, cfs).
  • Roughness n dominates the uncertainty. A concrete-lined channel is predictable to a few percent; a grass or riprap channel varies with season, maintenance, and depth of flow.
  • Supercritical flow (Fr > 1) in a lined channel brings hydraulic-jump and freeboard considerations this steady-uniform section calc does not cover — HEC-15 handles lined-channel design at those velocities.
  • Cross-check: identical trapezoidal inputs give the same A, P, R, V, and Q here as in the Manning's flow calculator — this tool reuses the same geometry and solver, and adds the picture.
