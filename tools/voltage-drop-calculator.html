How the Numbers Are Derived

The circular-mil method

Three phase: V_d = √3 × K × I × L / CM    Single phase: V_d = 2 × K × I × L / CM

K is conductor resistivity in Ω·cmil/ft (12.9 copper, 21.2 aluminum at operating temperature), L is one-way length in feet, CM is the conductor's circular-mil area. Parallel sets divide the current per conductor, equivalent to multiplying CM. This is the standard estimating method; it neglects reactance, which is conservative to ignore only at high power factor and modest conductor sizes.

The 3% / 5% guideline

The NEC's informational notes suggest limiting drop to 3% on a branch circuit or feeder and 5% total (source to load) for "reasonable efficiency of operation." It's a recommendation, not a code mandate — but motors, VFDs, and contactor coils get grumpy well before lights dim. Low voltage at a motor means higher current for the same load, which means more heat, which is how a marginal temp feeder cooks a rental pump motor over a week.
