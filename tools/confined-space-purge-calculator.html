Confined Space Ventilation Purge Calculator

Scheduling arithmetic for forced-air ventilation of manholes, wet wells, and vaults: purge time from space volume, required air changes, and blower output derated for duct losses. Runs entirely offline; inputs persist in this browser via localStorage.

This tool does not clear a space for entry. Ever. Only calibrated atmospheric testing (oxygen, LEL, H₂S, CO at minimum — top, middle, and bottom of the space) by the qualified person, under the entry permit, clears a space — and continuous monitoring keeps it cleared. A calculated purge time tells you when it's worth testing again, nothing more. Sewer-connected spaces can re-contaminate in minutes from upstream slugs regardless of how long the blower ran. The permit, the attendant, the retrieval equipment, and the competent/qualified person requirements are all upstream of this arithmetic.

How the Numbers Are Derived

The math

t = V × N / Q_eff — volume (cf) times required air changes divided by effective delivered airflow (CFM), giving minutes. Effective airflow is the rated CFM times the duct-loss factor. That's the whole calculation; everything hard about confined spaces is in the parts that aren't math.

Placement matters as much as time
  • Duct the supply to within a couple of feet of the bottom of the space (or the far end of a horizontal one) so fresh air displaces from the bottom up. A blower parked at the rim churns the top and leaves the bottom — where H₂S sits, heavier than air — undisturbed.
  • Position the blower intake upwind and away from generator exhaust, truck exhaust, and the space's own vent discharge, or you're pumping CO in with great efficiency.
  • Sewer-connected structures: plug or block channels where the entry procedure allows, and expect the atmosphere to change when upstream flow changes — which is what the continuous monitor is for.
  • If the pre-entry test fails after a full calculated purge, the space has a supply problem (off-gassing, connected atmosphere), not a time problem — more minutes on the blower isn't the fix; the entry supervisor's re-evaluation is.
