How the Numbers Are Derived

Tile math (Web Mercator / EPSG:3857)

x = ⌊(lon+180)/360 · 2^z⌋, y = ⌊(1 − ln(tan φ + sec φ)/π)/2 · 2^z⌋ — the standard OSM slippy scheme (origin top-left, y increasing southward). The inverse gives each tile's NW corner; tile (x+1, y+1) corners give the rest of the bbox. Web Mercator is defined on a sphere of radius 6,378,137 m and cuts off at ±85.0511° — the latitude where the world map becomes square.

Resolution and scale

Ground resolution = 156,543.034 · cos(lat) / 2^z meters per pixel (256-px tiles) — the cos(lat) is why a zoom level covers less ground per pixel in California than at the equator. "Map scale" for a screen is approximate by nature; the value shown assumes 96 dpi (scale ≈ resolution · 96 / 0.0254). High-DPI displays and retina @2x tiles halve the effective resolution per CSS pixel.

Field-mapping notes
  • z17–z19 is the useful range for utility-scale OSM editing — at z19 in Southern California a pixel is roughly a couple of inches, comfortably below aerial-imagery alignment error, which is the real accuracy floor.
  • The Overpass bbox order is (south, west, north, east) — different from the W,S,E,N most tools use. This page prints both so the comma-shuffling bug happens to someone else.
  • Tile y counts from the top (OSM/XYZ). TMS counts from the bottom: y_tms = 2^z − 1 − y. If your tiles render upside-down-and-mirrored, that's the flag to flip.
  • Bulk-downloading tiles from the OSM servers violates the tile usage policy — bbox math is for scoping surveys and Overpass queries, not for scraping.
