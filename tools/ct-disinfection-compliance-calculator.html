This calculator does not certify compliance. The state primacy agency and the system's approved monitoring plan certify compliance — this tool only does the arithmetic on numbers you supply. How the Numbers Are Derived

CT-achieved and T10

CT-achieved = C · T10, with residual C in mg/L and T10 in minutes. T10 is the detention time the fastest 10% of water experiences: T10 = (contact volume ÷ peak hourly flow) × baffling factor. In consistent field units, T10 (min) = volume (gal) ÷ flow (gpm) × BF. The baffling factor is the ratio T10/T that accounts for short-circuiting — 1.0 only for true plug flow like a pipe.

Ratios, sequential segments, and log inactivation

For each segment the inactivation ratio is CT-achieved ÷ CT-required, where CT-required is the value you read from the SWTR table for the log target and conditions. A segment that delivers the full required CT has a ratio of 1.0. Sequential segments (e.g. a pipeline, then a clearwell, then the distribution entry point) add their ratios, and compliance requires Σ ratios ≥ 1.0. The effective log inactivation achieved is log-target × Σ ratios — so a summed ratio of 1.0 exactly meets the target log.

Why this tool makes you enter CT-required

The SWTR CT tables are large lookup tables that vary steeply and non-linearly with temperature, pH, and residual. Transcribing them from memory into a calculator is how subtle errors get institutionalized. So this tool asks you to read each CT-required straight from the governing table for the exact cell your conditions fall in, and it does the ratio arithmetic on that. The conditions fields above exist only to make sure you pull the right cell — and to put those conditions on the printout.

Field notes
  • Peak hourly flow governs T10, not average day — the worst case is the busiest hour, when contact time is shortest.
  • Baffling-factor presets are for planning. A tracer study (step-dose or slug) measures the real T10/T for a specific basin at a specific level; use it for reporting where it exists.
  • Measure the residual at the outlet of the segment it credits — the concentration that survived the contact, not what went in.
  • Temperature is the big lever. The same basin that passes in August can fail in February because required CT roughly doubles as water goes cold — profile the cold-water condition.
  • Filtration provides most of the Giardia/virus credit; disinfection typically covers the remainder. Set the log target to the disinfection portion your plan assigns, not the whole plant requirement.
