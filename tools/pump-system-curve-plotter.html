How the Numbers Are Derived

Fitting the pump curve

A centrifugal pump's head-capacity curve is well approximated by a quadratic, H = a + bQ + cQ². The tool least-squares fits that through your entered points (at least three; shutoff and a far-right point pin it down). a is the shutoff head. For N pumps in parallel, the combined curve carries total flow Q at the head one pump makes at Q/N — parallel pumps add flow at equal head.

The system curve and the operating point

The system curve is Hsys = Hstatic + K·Qn: a fixed lift plus friction that grows with flow (n = 2 for Darcy, ~1.85 for Hazen-Williams). K comes from one design point, K = Hf / Qdesignn, or is entered directly. The operating point is the single flow where the pump curve and system curve cross — found here by bisection, since the pump head falls and the system head rises, giving exactly one intersection when the pump can overcome the static head.

Field notes
  • The manufacturer curve is at a specific speed and impeller diameter. A trimmed impeller or a VFD shifts it — scale it with the affinity laws first, then intersect.
  • Run two system curves: clean/new (low K) and aged/fouled (high K, valves throttled). The operating point walks left up the pump curve as the system stiffens — often into a worse-efficiency, higher-vibration zone.
  • Parallel pumps never double the flow. Because the system curve steepens with flow, two pumps might deliver only ~1.4× one pump's flow — the plot shows exactly how much you actually gain.
  • An operating point far right of best-efficiency (flat system, oversized pump) risks cavitation and motor overload; far left risks recirculation and low flow. Check it lands near BEP, and confirm NPSH margin separately.
