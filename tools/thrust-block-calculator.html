How the Numbers Are Derived

Thrust and bearing area

Internal pressure pushes on the pipe's cross-sectional area, A = π/4 · OD². The unbalanced thrust depends on the fitting: a bend is T = 2 · P · A · sin(θ/2), a dead end, cap, or tee branch is T = P · A, and a reducer is T = P · (A₁ − A₂). With P in psi and A in square inches, T comes out directly in pounds. The block's bearing area is Ab = T · SF / Sb, where Sb is the soil's safe bearing value in psf and SF the safety factor — giving Ab in square feet.

Field notes
  • The thrust acts along the bisector of the bend, outward. The block goes on the outside of the bend, poured against undisturbed trench wall — never against backfill, which hasn't the bearing to resist anything.
  • Size for test pressure, not working pressure. The joint sees its highest load during the hydrostatic test, and that's what has to be restrained.
  • Keep concrete off the joint and bolts. The fitting must stay accessible and repairable; wrap or form so the block bears on the fitting body, not the mechanical joint.
  • In poor soil — high water table, organics, disturbed fill — the safe bearing value approaches zero and blocks get absurdly large. That's the signal to switch to restrained joints, which carry the thrust in the pipe wall over a calculated length instead of into the soil face.
  • Vertical bends that thrust upward need a weight (gravity) block or restraint, not a bearing block — this tool sizes the horizontal bearing case.
