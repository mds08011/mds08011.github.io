How the Numbers Are Derived

The prism math

All volumes are the trench width × length × the relevant vertical slice, in bank (in-place) measure:

  • Excavation: W × D × L
  • Bedding: W × t_bed × L
  • Pipe zone: W × (OD + h_zone) × L − V_pipe, where the pipe displaces π/4 × (OD/12)² × L
  • Upper backfill: excavation − bedding slice − pipe zone slice

Conversion: 27 cf = 1 CY.

Bank vs. loose vs. compacted — the three cubic yards

The same dirt is three different volumes, and mixing them up is how orders go wrong:

  • Bank CY: in the ground, undisturbed — what the neat-line numbers are.
  • Loose CY: in the truck — bank × (1 + swell). This is what the hauling gets priced and scheduled on.
  • Compacted CY: back in the trench at spec density — for import, order more than neat-line because the loose delivered yard compacts smaller and some gets wasted. That's the compaction/waste allowance.

Spoil to haul: if native is reused above the pipe zone, the spoil is the material displaced by pipe + bedding + pipe-zone import (in bank measure), swelled. If everything above the pipe zone is also imported, the entire excavation hauls off.

Field notes
  • Depth varies along the run — for a long segment on grade, use the average of station depths, or split the run into reaches and run this per reach.
  • Trench boxes typically need 6–12 in. beyond their outside walls; if the box sets the dig width, use the box width plus over-cut, not the spec minimum.
  • Unsuitable subgrade at the bottom means over-excavation and extra bedding rock — a fast re-run of this tool with the deeper numbers is the change-order quantity backup.
  • Supplier tickets are in tons; inspectors think in CY; the trench is in bank measure. Keeping the three straight on the daily report saves the argument at the pay app.
