How the Numbers Are Derived

The curve shapes

Projected billing spreads the contract value over the duration by a cumulative fraction f(t), t = m / T. The smoothstep S-curve is f(t) = 3t² − 2t³ — slow start, steep middle, slow finish (f(0)=0, f(0.5)=0.5, f(1)=1), so the monthly amounts are bell-shaped. Front-loaded uses f = 2t − t², back-loaded f = t², linear f = t. Each month's billing is (f(m/T) − f((m−1)/T)) × value, and the monthly amounts sum exactly to the contract value.

Retention, lag, and the received line

Each progress payment pays (1 − retention) × billing, shifted later by the payment lag. The withheld retention accumulates and is released as a lump sum N months after the final billing month — the late jump you see in the received line. The peak financing gap is the largest value of cumulative billed − cumulative received: the money you've earned but not yet collected, which is the exposure this simple model shows. Peak retention held is retention × total billed, reached just before release.

Field notes
  • This is revenue timing only. Your real cash position also depends on when you pay labor (weekly), suppliers (net 30), and subs (often pay-when-paid). Retention you hold on your subs partially offsets the retention the owner holds on you — a real model nets the two.
  • Front-loading the schedule of values is common, visible, and a negotiation with the owner's rep. Mobilization line items exist for a reason; padding early activities that aren't really done is a different thing and gets caught.
  • Stored-materials billing changes both the curve and the paperwork — you can bill for material on site ahead of installation, but expect to document it and insure it.
  • The retention tail is real money sitting for months after you finish. On thin margins, the release date is a line on the cash forecast, not an afterthought.
