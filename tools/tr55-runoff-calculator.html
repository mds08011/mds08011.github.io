How the Numbers Are Derived

The runoff equation

Q = (P − Iₐ)² / (P − Iₐ + S), with S = 1000/CN − 10 and Iₐ = 0.2S (all inches). If P ≤ Iₐ, runoff is zero — the storm never exceeds the initial abstraction. Volume = depth × area (1 inch over 1 acre = 3,630 cf ≈ 27,154 gal; 12 acre-inches = 1 acre-ft).

Compositing caveats (the fine print that changes answers)
  • Area-weighting CN is standard but not always right. TR-55 itself warns that compositing pervious and impervious CNs can misstate runoff when the pervious CN is low or impervious areas are unconnected (drain across lawn before reaching the system) — Figures 2-3/2-4 in TR-55 adjust for that. When precision matters, compute runoff separately per cover and sum the volumes.
  • ARC/AMC: Table values are average antecedent runoff condition (ARC II). Wet (III) or dry (I) conversions exist but most design criteria just use II — follow the manual.
  • Low CN + small storm: the method is unreliable when Q computes to a few hundredths of an inch — real-world scatter swamps the estimate.
Where the inputs come from
  • P: NOAA Atlas 14 Precipitation-Frequency Data Server for the design return period, 24-hr duration.
  • HSG: NRCS Web Soil Survey — draw the site, read the hydrologic soil group per map unit. Dual classifications (e.g. A/D) use the drained/undrained letter per the drainage condition.
  • CN: TR-55 Table 2-2 (built into the presets here) or the local manual's table.
