Flood Elevation & Freeboard Checker

Line-item elevation compliance against the base flood elevation plus required freeboard — finished floors, equipment pads, electrical gear, wet well rims. AE (BFE-based) and AO (depth-based) modes. Runs entirely offline; inputs persist in this browser via localStorage.

Use limits — and the datum trap: Every elevation in this check must be on the same vertical datum. FIRMs published over the decades mix NGVD29 and NAVD88, which differ by roughly 2–2.5 ft in Southern California — comfortably more than most freeboard requirements, meaning a datum mixup can flip a pass to a fail (or worse, a fail to a paper pass). Check the FIRM panel's datum note against the survey's. The flood zone, BFE, and required freeboard come from the effective FIRM/FIS and the local floodplain ordinance (which often exceeds NFIP minimums); this tool just does the arithmetic.
Flood Basis

NFIP minimum for AO is depth + (locally required freeboard); many California communities require 1–2 ft above BFE. The ordinance governs.

How the Numbers Are Derived

The arithmetic (and where each number comes from)

AE/numbered A zones: required = BFE + freeboard. AO zones (shallow sheet flow, no BFE): required = highest adjacent grade + depth number + freeboard. Margin = proposed − required. The arithmetic is trivial by design — the whole game is the inputs: the effective FIRM panel and FIS profile for BFE (interpolate between cross-sections on the profile where the panel shows a range), the community's floodplain ordinance for freeboard, and a surveyed elevation on the right datum for the proposed value.

Zone cheat sheet
  • AE / A1–A30: 1% annual chance with BFE published. The workhorse zone for this check.
  • A (unnumbered): 1% annual chance, no BFE — the community or a study establishes one; don't guess.
  • AO: shallow flooding 1–3 ft, published as a depth number instead of an elevation.
  • AH: shallow ponding with a BFE — treat like AE.
  • VE: coastal wave action — freeboard measured to the lowest horizontal structural member, plus construction requirements far beyond this tool.
  • X (shaded/unshaded): outside the 1% floodplain — but critical facilities are often held to the 0.2% (500-yr) event by policy.
Why utility equipment keeps failing this check

Buildings get designed to the flood ordinance; the electrical gear, generators, and control panels get placed by whoever had space on the site plan. NFIP and most ordinances require utilities and service equipment elevated (or dry-floodproofed) to the same standard as the lowest floor — and a lift station whose motor control center goes underwater at the design flood is a lift station that fails exactly when the wet weather flows peak. Elevation certificates (FEMA form) document the as-built answer; this tool is for catching the problem while it's still a markup.
