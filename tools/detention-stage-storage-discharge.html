How the Numbers Are Derived

Average-end-area storage

Between two stages with surface areas A1 and A2 a vertical distance Δh apart, the incremental storage is the conic (average-end-area) volume ΔV = (A1 + A2)/2 · Δh. Cumulative storage sums these from the lowest stage up. On a straight-walled prism (constant area) this returns exactly A · h; on a concave bowl it slightly overestimates — acceptable for planning, and the stage-area survey is the real accuracy limiter, not the method.

Outlet discharge equations

With head h measured above the outlet's invert/crest:

Orifice: Q = Cd · A · √(2g·h), Cd default 0.6, A from the orifice diameter, g = 32.174 ft/s², head taken to the orifice centerline. Rectangular weir: Q = Cw · L · h^1.5, Cw default 3.33. 90° V-notch: Q = 2.49 · h^2.48. Total discharge at a stage is the sum over outlets that are flowing at that stage. (The sibling weir & orifice tool uses Cd 0.61 for a sharp-edged orifice; use your structure's tested coefficient.)

Field notes
  • This is the input to routing, not routing itself. The storage-indication (modified Puls) method takes this 2S/Δt + Q relationship and marches an inflow hydrograph through it to get the peak stage and outflow.
  • A submerged outlet transitions from weir to orifice behavior; if downstream tailwater can rise above an outlet, the free-discharge equations here overstate flow. The tool warns when a lower outlet sits below a higher one's invert.
  • Clogging is the unmodeled failure mode. Trash racks, a minimum orifice size (many agencies floor it near 3 in.), and a factor of safety on the primary outlet are how it's handled — not in the hydraulics.
  • The stage-area survey is where the accuracy lives. A basin graded off a rough plan and one shot as-built can differ enough to change the routed peak.
