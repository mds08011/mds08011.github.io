Earned Value S-Curve Calculator

June 2026

This calculator plots a cumulative Earned Value Management (EVM) S-curve (a graph of cumulative project value over time, named for its typical S-shape as spending starts slowly, accelerates, and tapers off) and computes standard schedule and cost performance metrics. You enter the Budget at Completion (BAC, total planned project budget in dollars) and per-period dollar amounts for Planned Value (PV, budgeted cost of work scheduled), Earned Value (EV, budgeted cost of work completed), and Actual Cost (AC, actual money spent). The page accumulates each series and evaluates performance at the data date (the last period containing an EV entry) [1].

Using cumulative PV, EV, and AC through the data date (all in dollars), the calculator computes Schedule Variance SV = EV − PV (dollars) and Cost Variance CV = EV − AC (dollars), where positive values indicate ahead-of-schedule or under-budget performance [1]. The dimensionless performance indices are Schedule Performance Index SPI = EV / PV and Cost Performance Index CPI = EV / AC, where 1.00 is on plan, values above 1.00 are favorable, and values below 1.00 are unfavorable (the table highlights indices below 0.95 in red and between 0.95 and 1.00 in amber). Percent complete is EV / BAC.

For cost forecasting, the tool uses the standard CPI-based chain: Estimate at Completion EAC = BAC / CPI (projected final cost in dollars, assuming current cost efficiency continues), Estimate to Complete ETC = EAC − AC (projected remaining cost in dollars), and Variance at Completion VAC = BAC − EAC (projected final underrun or overrun in dollars) [1][2].

Assumptions and limits
  • Empirical EVM studies show that cumulative CPI tends to stabilize after roughly 20% project completion, so early cost overruns are generally a strong indicator of final cost performance [1][2].
  • SPI converges back toward 1.00 near project completion even on a late project, because cumulative EV eventually equals BAC once all planned work is finished. Late-stage schedule status should be checked against actual milestone dates rather than SPI alone [1].
  • EVM results depend on how EV is measured. Earning value from physical quantities in place (installed units multiplied by budgeted unit rates) keeps EV consistent with PV, whereas subjective percent-complete estimates can stall near 90% while actual costs continue to climb.
  • The calculator implements EAC = BAC / CPI. Other methods, such as the composite formula EAC = AC + (BAC − EV) / (CPI × SPI) or a bottom-up ETC (re-estimating remaining work item by item), may be required by contract or a Project Management Office (PMO) for official reporting [2].
  • The page runs offline in the browser and stores entered periods in localStorage (browser-side local storage).
