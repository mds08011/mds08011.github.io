Checking Crane Picks Against Chart Capacity

May 2026

Overview

Before lifting equipment with a mobile crane, lift planners compare the total suspended weight (gross load) against the rated capacity listed on the crane's load chart at the working radius [1][2]. Working radius is the horizontal distance from the crane's center of rotation to the hook. This calculator sums the gross load components, computes percent of chart capacity at both the pick radius (where the load is lifted) and the set radius (where it is placed), and checks the governing ratio against a user-chosen critical-lift threshold.

Formulas

The calculation sums the weight components and divides by the chart capacity at the governing radius:

W_gross = W_load + W_rigging + W_block + W_deductions
Pct_cap = (W_gross / C_chart) × 100

Here W_gross is total gross load in pounds (lb), W_load is the equipment or material weight in lb (including any shipping skids lifted with it), W_rigging is the weight of slings, shackles, and any spreader bar (lifting beam) in lb, W_block is the weight of the hook block or headache ball (weighted overhaul ball on the hoist line) in lb, W_deductions covers other chart deductions such as a stowed or erected jib in lb, C_chart is the crane's rated chart capacity at the working radius in lb, and Pct_cap is percent of chart capacity in percent. Because crane capacity decreases as radius increases, the tool checks both pick and set radii (entered in feet) and reports whichever gives the higher percentage.

Project specifications and safety programs commonly require a formal written critical-lift plan above a set threshold (often 75% of chart capacity), as well as for two-crane lifts or lifts over energized power lines or occupied buildings. This tool only screens the capacity ratio and does not replace a lift plan or a qualified lift director's review. The governing document for any lift is always the manufacturer's load chart in the cab of the specific crane, including its General Notes and deduction tables [1][2].

Assumptions and limits
  • Chart capacities depend on the exact crane configuration, including boom length, counterweight (ballast installed on the crane), outrigger extension (full versus intermediate stabilizing beams), and swing zone (such as over-the-rear versus 360 degrees).
  • Working radius is measured from the crane's center of rotation to the hook rather than from the outriggers or tracks, and increases slightly as the boom deflects under load.
  • Equipment weights should be verified from shipping papers (bill of lading), manufacturer submittals (equipment data sheets), or a scale rather than estimated from memory.
  • This tool does not check ground bearing pressure under the outrigger floats (pads) or crane matting (timber or steel pads used to spread loads on soft soil), nor does it evaluate wind limits, rigging capacity, or multi-crane lifts.

The calculator runs offline in a single HTML file using plain JavaScript with no external libraries, and saves your inputs in your browser's local storage (localStorage).
