Flood Elevation and Freeboard Checker

July 2026

This calculator checks proposed building and utility elevations (such as a finished floor, electrical service, generator pad, or pump-station wet well rim) against a required flood protection elevation. In Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) flood zones, the required minimum elevation is based on the Base Flood Elevation (BFE, the 1%-annual-chance flood water surface) or a published shallow flood depth from the effective Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) and Flood Insurance Study (FIS) [1], plus any required freeboard (an extra vertical safety margin set by local floodplain ordinances).

For Zone AE and numbered A zones (1%-annual-chance flood zones with a published BFE), the tool calculates:

E_req = BFE + F   and   M = E_prop − E_req

For Zone AO (shallow sheet-flow flood zones with a published flood depth rather than a BFE), the tool calculates:

E_req = HAG + D + F   and   M = E_prop − E_req

Here E_req is the required minimum elevation (ft), BFE is the base flood elevation (ft), F is the required freeboard (ft), HAG is the highest adjacent grade at the structure (ft), D is the published AO depth number (ft), E_prop is the proposed elevation of each line item (ft), and M is the resulting margin (ft, where zero or positive passes and negative fails).

Every elevation entered in the check must reference the same vertical datum (the zero-elevation reference surface used by a survey or map). Older FEMA FIRMs and FIS profiles sometimes publish BFEs on the National Geodetic Vertical Datum of 1929 (NGVD29), while modern site surveys typically use the North American Vertical Datum of 1988 (NAVD88). In Southern California, NGVD29 and NAVD88 differ by roughly 2 to 2.5 ft, which exceeds typical 1 to 2 ft freeboard requirements. Mixing datums produces an incorrect pass or fail result. Datum conversions can be checked with the National Geodetic Survey (NGS) VDatum tool and published benchmark datasheets [3].

Assumptions and limits
  • Under National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) criteria in 44 CFR §60.3, electrical, heating, ventilation, plumbing, and other service equipment must be elevated or designed to prevent floodwater entry during the base flood, and many local ordinances hold utility equipment to the same elevation as the lowest floor [2]. Dry-floodproofing (sealing walls and openings to keep water out) is permitted only where allowed by the local ordinance for eligible non-residential structures. Checking equipment such as a sewage lift station motor control center, generator pad, or wet well rim (the top opening of the underground collection chamber) during design helps catch conflicts before as-built survey documentation on a FEMA Elevation Certificate.
  • The tool displays a caution when the smallest passing margin is below 0.3 ft, because combined survey and construction grading tolerances can use up a thin margin.
  • The effective FIRM panel, FIS profile [1], and the local community's floodplain ordinance govern flood zones, BFEs, freeboard, and special requirements (such as coastal Zone VE wave rules, which this tool does not cover).
  • The calculator is a single offline HTML file with no external dependencies. Inputs are stored in browser localStorage (saved only on your device) until cleared.
