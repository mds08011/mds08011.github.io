Bearing and Distance Traverse Calculator

June 2026

This calculator checks a metes-and-bounds land description (a boundary written as a chain of directional segments, called calls, starting from a point of beginning, or POB). Each leg is entered as a quadrant bearing (northeast NE, southeast SE, southwest SW, or northwest NW, in degrees, minutes, and seconds from 0° to 90°) and a distance in feet. The page computes the running northing (N, north-south coordinate in feet) and easting (E, east-west coordinate in feet) at each corner, the closure error (the straight-line gap between the final point and the starting point), the precision ratio, and the enclosed area in square feet (sf) and acres (ac).

Each quadrant bearing angle θ (in degrees) is converted to a clockwise azimuth az measured from north: az = θ for NE, 180° − θ for SE, 180° + θ for SW, and 360° − θ for NW [1]. Each leg then advances the coordinates by ΔN = d · cos(az) and ΔE = d · sin(az), where d is the leg length in feet and ΔN and ΔE are the northing and easting increments in feet [1]. Closure error is e = √(ΔNclose² + ΔEclose²) in feet, where ΔNclose and ΔEclose are the coordinate gaps from the final corner back to the POB, and the precision ratio is P = L / e (reported as 1:P), where L is the total perimeter in feet [1]. Polygon area is computed on the closed vertex list using the planar shoelace formula A = 0.5 · |Σ(Ei · Ni+1 − Ei+1 · Ni)| in square feet [1].

If a geographic anchor latitude (φ, degrees) and longitude (degrees) are entered for the POB, the calculator maps the planar offsets to geographic coordinates on a local tangent plane using Δlat = ΔNm / M(φ) and Δlon = ΔEm / (N(φ) · cos(φ)) (in radians, then converted to degrees), where ΔNm and ΔEm are offsets from the POB in meters converted using the US survey foot (1200 / 3937 m/ft), M(φ) is the meridional radius of curvature in meters, and N(φ) is the prime vertical radius of curvature in meters on the GRS80 (Geodetic Reference System 1980) ellipsoid [2]. The resulting polygon can be copied as GeoJSON (a JSON format for geographic features) or WKT (Well-Known Text, a plain-text geometry format) for viewing in desktop mapping tools such as QGIS, OpenStreetMap editors such as JOSM, or the GeoJSON and WKT Converter.

Assumptions and limits
  • In standard surveying practice, modern boundary traverses commonly close at 1:10,000 or better, while older deeds often close around 1:5,000 [1]. A precision ratio worse than about 1:3,000 frequently indicates a transcription mistake such as a transposed digit, a skipped leg, or an inverted quadrant.
  • This calculator checks arithmetic only and is not a land survey. The governing deed or record map and its basis-of-bearings note (which defines whether bearings refer to grid north, true north, or a record line) control legal orientation.
  • Curved boundary segments are not modeled directly. Entering a curve by its chord bearing and chord distance closes the endpoints correctly, but understates perimeter length and alters the computed area slightly.
  • The local tangent-plane conversion is accurate to well under a centimeter over typical parcel extents (roughly a mile or less), so exported map placement depends mainly on the accuracy of the anchor coordinates entered.
  • The tool runs offline in the browser and saves the current traverse in localStorage (browser-side local storage).
