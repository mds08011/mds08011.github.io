Estimating Detention Basin Storage with the Modified Rational Method

June 2026

This calculator gives a preliminary storage volume estimate for a stormwater detention basin (a pond or storage area that temporarily holds runoff and releases it at a controlled rate). When sizing a storm drain pipe for peak flow, engineers evaluate a storm equal to the watershed's time of concentration, Tc (the time it takes runoff to travel from the hydraulically farthest point of the drainage area to the outlet, in minutes). For detention storage, however, a longer storm often produces a larger required volume because total runoff volume keeps growing faster than the outlet can release it [2].

In its main mode, the tool uses the Modified Rational method to sweep 400 trial storm durations D (minutes) from Tc up to a chosen maximum duration. At each duration it computes rainfall intensity i (inches per hour, in/hr) from a local Intensity-Duration-Frequency (IDF) equation i = a / (D + b)ⁿ using local storm coefficients a, b, and n, then calculates peak inflow Q_in = C·i·A in cubic feet per second (cfs), where C is the dimensionless post-development runoff coefficient (between 0 and 1) and A is the drainage area in acres (using the Rational method approximation that 1 acre-inch per hour is about 1 cfs). Required storage is then computed with a rectangular inflow approximation (assuming constant inflow and constant allowable outlet release Q_allow in cfs over the duration D, with 60 converting minutes to seconds) [2]:

V(D) = (Q_in − Q_allow) · D · 60  (cubic feet)

The tool finds the storm duration that maximizes V(D) (the critical duration), displays the result in acre-feet, cubic feet, and gallons, and prints a summary table across the sweep range. It flags when the peak sits at the upper end of the sweep range (meaning the maximum duration should be increased) or when inflow never exceeds Q_allow (for example, if Q_allow was entered in gallons per minute instead of cfs).

A second mode computes a simple volume difference, V = V_post − V_pre, subtracting pre-development runoff volume from post-development runoff volume for the same storm (in acre-feet or cubic feet). That mode works with volumes from the companion TR-55 runoff calculator, which uses the Natural Resources Conservation Service Technical Release 55 method and Curve Number (CN, an index of soil and land-cover runoff potential) [1]. Some local drainage standards require peak-rate detention, some require capturing the added runoff volume, and some require the larger of the two.

Assumptions and limits
  • This is a preliminary screening tool, not final basin design. Local county or city hydrology manuals specify the governing method, design storms, IDF coefficients, allowable release rates, and safety factors.
  • A real outlet structure (such as an orifice opening or overflow weir) discharges less at low water depth (stage, or head) than at full depth, so assuming full Q_allow for the entire storm can underestimate required volume compared to full reservoir routing (simulating a time-varying inflow hydrograph through the basin's actual graded stage-storage curve and outlet rating curve) [2].
  • This sweep does not check multi-storm performance (for example, 2-, 10-, and 100-year storms together), maximum drawdown time (how quickly the basin must drain after a storm), freeboard (extra vertical height above the design water surface), or emergency spillway capacity.

The calculator runs in the browser and saves your last entered inputs in browser storage (localStorage).
