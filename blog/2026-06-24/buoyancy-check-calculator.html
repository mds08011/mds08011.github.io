Structure Buoyancy and Flotation Check Calculator

June 2026

This calculator performs a flotation screening check for an empty below-grade rectangular or circular concrete structure, such as a basin, wet well (an underground chamber that holds water or wastewater for pumping), tank, or vault, when groundwater is present around the base. It compares the downward dead load (permanent weight of the structure and soil cover) against the upward hydrostatic buoyant force to compute the factor of safety (FS) against uplift [1].

Buoyant uplift force and factor of safety are calculated as U = Afootprint × hgw × γw and FS = Wresist / U, where U is upward buoyant force in pounds (lb), Afootprint is outside plan footprint area in square feet (ft²), hgw is groundwater height above the bottom of the base slab in feet (capped at the total structure height), γw = 62.4 pounds per cubic foot (pcf) is the unit weight of fresh water, and Wresist is total permanent resisting weight in pounds. The resisting weight sums concrete dead load (estimated from wall, base slab, and optional top slab dimensions, or entered directly), soil cover weight over a buried top slab, and any additional permanent weight.

The calculator also inverts the equation to report the maximum groundwater height for a chosen target factor of safety (commonly 1.10 to 1.25 depending on project specifications and design basis) [2]: hmax = Wresist / (FSreq × Afootprint × 62.4), where hmax is the maximum groundwater height in feet above the bottom of the base slab and FSreq is the required factor of safety.

Assumptions and limits
  • The check assumes the structure is completely empty, since draining a basin or tank removes the stabilizing weight of its liquid contents [1].
  • Side-wall friction and soil adhesion (shear resistance along the exterior walls) are neglected because they are unreliable in soft or recently disturbed backfill (soil placed around a buried structure).
  • Tension piles, rock anchors, and hydrostatic pressure-relief valves are outside the scope of this screening check and require structural design [1].
  • If soil cover over a buried top slab sits below the groundwater table, enter its buoyant unit weight (saturated unit weight minus 62.4 pcf) rather than its total unit weight.
  • Interim construction conditions (for example, after backfilling a vault but before the top slab, pavement, or heavy equipment is installed) and temporary perched water tables (rainwater trapped in permeable gravel backfill around walls) can govern over the completed condition. Design groundwater levels should come from the project geotechnical report, and pumping limits for dewatering (lowering groundwater with well points or sumps) should follow the approved dewatering plan.
  • The tool runs offline in the browser and stores inputs in localStorage (browser-side local storage).

The tool: Structure Buoyancy / Flotation Check.

References

  1. ACI 350-20, Code Requirements for Environmental Engineering Concrete Structures, American Concrete Institute (load combinations and uplift provisions).
  2. ACI 350.4R-04, Design Considerations for Environmental Engineering Concrete Structures, American Concrete Institute (flotation guidance and typical factors of safety).
