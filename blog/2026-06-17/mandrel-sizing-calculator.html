Sizing Deflection Test Mandrels from the Base Inside Diameter

June 2026

Flexible gravity sewer pipes such as PVC (polyvinyl chloride) and HDPE (high-density polyethylene) rely on surrounding bedding and embedment (granular material placed under and around the pipe) to hold their circular shape under soil loads. Acceptance specifications check installation quality by pulling a mandrel (a rigid cylindrical metal gauge, usually with 9 longitudinal legs) through the finished line to confirm ring deflection stays within an allowable limit. This calculator computes the required mandrel contact diameter from the pipe's standard base inside diameter (base ID), and also checks what deflection percentage an existing mandrel verifies.

The required diameter is D_mandrel = ID_base × (1 − d / 100), and the inverse check is d = (1 − D_mandrel / ID_base) × 100, where D_mandrel is the required or measured mandrel contact diameter in inches, ID_base is the pipe's standard base inside diameter in inches, and d is the allowable or verified ring deflection in percent (for example, 5 for 5%). Base ID is not the nominal pipe size. It is the average inside diameter defined by the pipe standard, calculated from the nominal outside diameter (OD) minus two average wall thicknesses with manufacturing tolerances applied. For 8-inch ASTM D3034 SDR 35 PVC pipe (Standard Dimension Ratio 35, where OD divided by minimum wall thickness equals 35), the base ID is 7.665 in., giving a 5% mandrel diameter of 7.282 in. [1]. Multiplying a nominal 8.00 in. diameter by 0.95 gives 7.60 in., which oversizes the mandrel and rejects pipe within tolerance.

To use the calculator, pick an ASTM D3034 SDR 35 preset (6 to 15 in.) from the Base ID menu [1], or leave it on custom entry and enter the base ID from the pipe standard or manufacturer submittal (product data sheet) for ASTM F679 large-diameter PVC [2], profile-wall pipe (ribbed or corrugated wall pipe), or HDPE. Choose the allowable deflection (5%, 7.5%, or Custom). To run the inverse check, type a measured diameter into the optional Actual mandrel diameter on hand field. The calculator reports the deflection percentage verified by that mandrel and warns if it is looser or stricter than the target limit. Inputs persist in browser localStorage (local storage on your device) and can be cleared with Clear all inputs.

Assumptions and limits
  • Rigid mandrels typically have an odd number of legs (commonly 9) so two legs cannot sit directly opposite each other across a single flattened axis. Standard practice is to pull the mandrel by hand without winches or mechanical pulling equipment.
  • Specifications commonly require waiting at least 30 days after final backfill (soil refilled over the trench) before deflection testing so initial embedment settlement and pipe deflection develop first [3].
  • Checking the mandrel against a proving ring (a machined calibration ring) or micrometer before testing confirms that the legs are not bent out of tolerance.
  • Failed pipe sections typically require excavating and re-compacting the embedment and haunching (the compacted material supporting the lower sides of the pipe) [3].
  • Where contract specifications include a mandrel table, deflection limit, or wait period, the contract specifications govern over this calculator.
