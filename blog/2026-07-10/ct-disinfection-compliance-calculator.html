CT Disinfection Compliance Calculator

July 2026

Under the U.S. Surface Water Treatment Rule (SWTR, 40 CFR Part 141 Subpart H), drinking-water disinfection performance is evaluated using CT (Concentration times Time, in mg·min/L) [1][2]. This calculator computes the achieved CT for one or more sequential disinfection segments (such as a contact pipeline followed by a clearwell, or treated-water storage basin), divides each segment's achieved CT by the required CT you look up from the regulatory tables, and sums the resulting inactivation ratios.

For each segment, the calculator uses three equations [1]:

  • T10 = (V / Q) × BF
  • CT_achieved = C · T10
  • Ratio = CT_achieved / CT_required

Here V is the active contact volume in gallons (gal), Q is the peak hourly flow rate in gallons per minute (gpm), BF is the dimensionless baffling factor (T10 / T, where T = V / Q is theoretical detention time in minutes), T10 is the contact time in minutes (min) that at least 90% of the water spends in the segment (the time for the fastest 10% to exit), C is the residual disinfectant concentration at the segment outlet in milligrams per liter (mg/L), and CT_required is the required CT in mg·min/L for the target pathogen and water conditions. Across sequential segments, the individual ratios add together, and compliance requires Σ Ratio ≥ 1.0 [1]. Effective log inactivation (base-10 pathogen reduction, where 1-log is 90% and 3-log is 99.9%) is computed as Log_target × Σ Ratio.

The SWTR CT lookup tables are not embedded in the page because required CT varies non-linearly with disinfectant, water temperature (°C), pH (for free chlorine), and residual concentration, and state primacy agencies (state drinking-water regulators enforcing federal rules) may adopt specific versions [1][2]. The conditions section records disinfectant, pathogen (Giardia, a waterborne protozoan parasite, or viruses), log target, temperature, and pH so you can pull the matching value from the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (USEPA) Guidance Manual tables [1] and keep those conditions visible on the calculation summary. An optional volume helper computes tank or pipe volume in gallons from dimensions. All inputs are saved in the browser's localStorage (local device storage) and the calculator runs offline.

Assumptions and limits
  • Peak hourly flow governs T10 under the SWTR rather than average daily flow because contact time is shortest during peak demand [1].
  • Typical USEPA Guidance Manual baffling factors are 0.1 (unbaffled basin), 0.3 (poor), 0.5 (average), 0.7 (superior), and 1.0 (plug flow in a pipe) to account for short-circuiting (water bypassing part of the tank volume) [1]. Planning presets are estimates, and a tracer study (measuring a benign chemical tracer through the basin) provides the actual T10 / T ratio for reporting where available.
  • Required CT increases sharply in cold water (roughly doubling from warm summer water to cold winter water for free chlorine), so cold-water conditions usually govern [1].
  • Measure residual C at the outlet of the segment being credited rather than at the chemical injection point [1].
  • Filtration normally provides part of the plant's total Giardia and virus credit, so set the log target to the disinfection portion assigned in the system's approved monitoring plan [2].
  • This calculator performs arithmetic only. Official compliance is determined by the state primacy agency under the system's approved monitoring plan and disinfection profiling and benchmarking records (historical CT tracking required under the SWTR) [2].
