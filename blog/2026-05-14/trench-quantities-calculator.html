Trench Quantities in Bank, Loose, and Compacted Yards

May 2026

Estimating earthwork for a pipeline trench starts with rectangular prism volumes and then converts those volumes across three soil states: in-ground bank cubic yards (CY, where 1 CY = 27 cubic feet), loose CY in haul trucks, and compacted CY placed back in the trench. The Trench Excavation and Backfill Quantity Calculator computes neat-line (theoretical rectangular trench) volumes for excavation, bedding (granular support below the pipe), pipe zone (fill around and immediately above the pipe), and upper backfill, then converts them into imported material tonnage, excess soil (spoil) haul-off volume, and truckload counts.

All neat-line volumes are computed in cubic feet (ft³) and divided by 27 to report bank CY:

  • Total excavation: V_exc = W × D × L, where W is trench width in feet (ft), D is average trench depth in ft, and L is trench length in ft.
  • Pipe displacement: V_pipe = (π/4) × (OD / 12)² × L, where OD is actual pipe outside diameter in inches (in), divided by 12 to convert to feet.
  • Bedding: V_bed = W × (t_bed / 12) × L, where t_bed is bedding depth below the pipe in inches (in).
  • Pipe zone (net of pipe): V_zone = W × ((OD + h_zone) / 12) × L − V_pipe, where h_zone is pipe-zone cover height above the top (crown) of the pipe in inches (in).
  • Upper backfill above pipe zone: V_upper = V_exc − V_bed − (V_zone + V_pipe).

Because soil expands when excavated and compacts when tamped back in place [1], the calculator separates ordering and hauling volumes from neat-line bank volumes:

  • Bank CY: undisturbed volume in the ground (the neat-line prism volumes above).
  • Loose CY (spoil haul-off): excavated soil bulks in the truck, computed as V_loose = V_bank × (1 + swell / 100), where swell is the percent swell factor (commonly 15% to 25% for typical soils) [1]. When native soil is reused above the pipe zone, bank spoil equals the volume displaced by the pipe, bedding, and pipe-zone import. When the upper zone is also imported, the entire excavation is hauled off.
  • Import order volume (compacted-equivalent): delivered loose aggregate shrinks when compacted to specified density and includes some placement waste. The tool applies a compaction and waste allowance percentage (compf) to neat-line import volume: V_order = V_import_neat × (1 + compf / 100).

Finally, ordered import CY is multiplied by material density in tons per CY (commonly 1.35 to 1.5 tons/CY for crushed rock or sand) to give total tons, and both import and loose spoil volumes are divided by truck capacity (CY per load, rounded up) to estimate truckloads. The page runs offline with no external dependencies, and inputs persist on your device in browser localStorage.

Assumptions and limits
  • Rectangular prism geometry: the tool assumes vertical trench walls at a single average depth. Where depth varies along a sloped pipeline, use the average of measured station depths or split the run into separate segments (reaches). Sloped or benched excavations and structures such as manholes or valve vaults must be added separately.
  • Actual dig width and pipe OD: if a trench box (a steel shoring shield) controls the excavation width, enter the box width plus typical over-cut (often 6 to 12 in) instead of the minimum trench width. Use actual pipe outside diameters from manufacturer dimension tables rather than nominal pipe sizes.
  • Unsuitable subgrade: if soft soil at the trench bottom requires extra excavation and additional bedding rock, re-running the calculator with the deeper trench and bedding values provides the incremental volume for documentation.
  • Measurement and payment: contract specifications govern trench details, allowable materials, and payable quantities, while supplier scale tickets govern billed tonnage.
