Hydrotest Makeup Water Calculator

July 2026

During a hydrostatic pressure test (hydrotest) on a gasketed-joint water main (a pressure pipe assembled with rubber-gasketed joints), the pipe is filled with water, pressurized, and held for a specified duration. A small volume of makeup water (also called the testing allowance) is commonly permitted to maintain test pressure as trapped air dissolves and rubber gaskets seat. This calculator computes the allowable makeup rate, the total allowance over the test duration, the rate per 1,000 ft of pipe, and an optional pass/fail check against measured makeup water.

The standard length-based testing allowance formula used in American Water Works Association (AWWA) standards C600 (ductile-iron pipe) and C605 (polyvinyl chloride, or PVC, pipe) is [1][2]:

Q = (S · D · √P) / 148,000 and V_allow = Q · t

Here Q is the allowable makeup rate in gallons per hour (gal/hr), S is the length of pipe tested in feet (ft), D is the nominal (designated) pipe diameter in inches (in), P is the average test pressure in pounds per square inch (psi), t is the test duration in hours (hr), and V_allow is the total allowable makeup volume in gallons (gal). For example, 1,000 ft of 12-inch pipe held at 150 psi average pressure gives Q = (1,000 · 12 · √150) / 148,000 = 0.993 gal/hr, or 1.99 gal over a 2-hour hold. If you enter an actual measured makeup volume V_meas (gal), the tool reports pass/fail and the margin V_allow − V_meas.

Some older specifications use 133,200 as the divisor instead of 148,000. Both constants come from the joint-count form of the same equation, Q = (N · D · √P) / 7,400, where N is the number of joints in the test section [1][2]. For 20-ft laying lengths (standard pipe stick length between joints), N = S / 20 and 20 × 7,400 = 148,000. For 18-ft laying lengths, N = S / 18 and 18 × 7,400 = 133,200, which gives about 11% more allowable makeup per foot. The calculator lets you select either divisor. Although AWWA C605 includes the same testing allowance formula [2], some project specifications require zero makeup water for gasketed PVC mains, so the calculator also provides a zero-allowance checkbox.

Project specifications govern the required test pressure, hold duration, divisor, and any zero-allowance rule. Inputs are saved in the browser's localStorage (local device storage) and the calculator runs offline.

Assumptions and limits
  • The testing allowance accounts for air absorption and gasket seating rather than active leaks. Under AWWA C600 and C605, any visible leak (such as a weeping joint) must be repaired and fails the test even if measured makeup is below the calculated limit [1][2].
  • Water temperature changes affect pressure inside a full, closed pipeline (roughly on the order of 1 psi per °F in a rigid line, depending on pipe material and restraint). Cooling drops pressure without water loss, while warming raises pressure and can mask a small leak. Recording water and ambient temperatures at the start and end of the hold helps interpret pressure changes.
  • Because allowable makeup volumes are small, measure added water with a calibrated container or pump flow meter rather than estimating from a supply tank level gauge.
  • Fill the pipe slowly, vent trapped air at high points, and let pressure stabilize before starting the timed hold.
