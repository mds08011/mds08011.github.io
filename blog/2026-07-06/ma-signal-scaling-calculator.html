4–20 mA Signal Scaling Calculator

July 2026

Industrial sensors often transmit measurements over a 4 to 20 milliamp (mA) current loop, where 4 mA represents the bottom of the calibrated range (a “live zero” so 0 mA indicates a broken circuit) and 20 mA represents the top of the range. This calculator converts between loop current, percent of span, and process variable (PV, the physical reading in engineering units such as ft, psi, or GPM) for instrument loop checks between field transmitters, Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs), and Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) displays.

In standard instrumentation terminology (ANSI/ISA-51.1), the Lower Range Value (LRV) is the engineering value at 4 mA, the Upper Range Value (URV) is the value at 20 mA, and the span is URV − LRV [1]. Setting a nonzero LRV handles elevated-zero and suppressed-zero ranges (such as vacuum or bidirectional flow). For linear signals (level, pressure, temperature, or pre-linearized flow meters):

P = ((I − 4) / 16) × 100,   PV = LRV + ((I − 4) / 16) × (URV − LRV),   I = 4 + 16 × (PV − LRV) / (URV − LRV)

Here I is loop current in milliamps (mA), P is percent of span (%), and PV, LRV, and URV are in the instrument's engineering units.

For differential-pressure (DP) flow meters such as an orifice plate or venturi tube, pressure drop varies with the square of flow rate. If the transmitter sends raw linear DP and square-root extraction is configured downstream in the PLC or SCADA system, flow is recovered as:

PV = LRV + √((I − 4) / 16) × (URV − LRV)

Under square-root scaling, 12 mA (50% signal) corresponds to 70.7% of full-scale flow and 8 mA (25% signal) corresponds to 50% of full-scale flow. Square-root extraction should be enabled in only one device (either the transmitter or the PLC/SCADA, never both). The calculator prints a five-point checkout table at 4, 8, 12, 16, and 20 mA (0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of span) for the selected linear or square-root characterization.

Under NAMUR NE 43, currents outside 4 to 20 mA distinguish valid over-range and under-range readings from hardware faults: 3.8 to 20.5 mA is valid measuring range with margin, ≤ 3.6 mA indicates a downscale (low) transmitter fault or wiring problem, ≥ 21.0 mA indicates an upscale (high) transmitter fault, and 0 mA indicates an open loop (broken wire, blown fuse, or dead power supply) [2]. The calculator flags any entered current that falls in these diagnostic bands.

Assumptions and limits
  • When a loop includes a precision 250-ohm (250 Ω) resistor (commonly wired at a PLC analog input), loop current can be checked without breaking the circuit by measuring DC voltage across the resistor and dividing by 250 Ω using Ohm's law (I = V / R, so 3.000 V / 250 Ω = 12.00 mA).
  • Confirm configured LRV, URV, and square-root extraction settings directly from the transmitter display or using HART (Highway Addressable Remote Transducer, a digital protocol carried on the 4 to 20 mA loop), as field settings can differ from earlier project loop sheets or instrument index lists.
  • If field and SCADA values disagree by a constant ratio across the five-point table, check whether the PLC analog input is scaled to 0 to 100% while SCADA expects engineering units (or vice versa).
  • Below roughly 10% signal on a square-root DP flow loop, small pressure errors cause large flow swings, so control systems typically apply a low-flow cutoff.
  • Approved project loop sheets and commissioning procedures govern actual loop checks. The calculator is a single offline HTML file with no external dependencies and stores inputs in browser localStorage (local storage on your device).
