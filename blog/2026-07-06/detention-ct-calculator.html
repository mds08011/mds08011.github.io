Detention Time and Disinfection CT Calculator

July 2026

This calculator estimates hydraulic retention time (HRT, the theoretical average time water spends in a tank or basin) and drinking-water disinfection CT (disinfectant concentration multiplied by contact time). In a clearwell (a finished-water storage and contact basin), some water short-circuits from inlet to outlet faster than the theoretical average. Under U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Surface Water Treatment Rule (SWTR) guidance, disinfection credit is based on T10, the contact time exceeded by 90% of the water passing through the basin (meaning the fastest 10% exits in T10 or less) [1].

The calculator evaluates the following equations:

HRT = V / Q,   T10 = HRT × BF,   CT = C × T10,   CT_ratio = CT / CT_req,   Q_max = (V × BF × C) / CT_req

Here V is effective basin water volume in gallons (gal, entered directly or calculated from rectangular or circular dimensions in feet using 7.4805 gal/ft^3), Q is flow rate in gallons per minute (GPM), HRT and T10 are in minutes (min), BF is the dimensionless baffling factor (T10 / HRT), C is the disinfectant residual measured at the segment outlet in milligrams per liter (mg/L), CT and CT_req are achieved and required contact-time products in mg·min/L, and Q_max is the maximum flow rate in GPM that still meets CT_req at the given volume, baffling factor, and residual.

The baffling condition dropdown provides the five standard EPA SWTR baffling factors: 0.1 for unbaffled or mixed basins, 0.3 for poor baffling, 0.5 for average baffling, 0.7 for superior baffling (perforated inlet, serpentine internal baffle walls that route water back and forth, and a weir or perforated launder outlet), and 1.0 for ideal plug flow (water moving uniformly through a pipeline with minimal mixing) [1]. State drinking water regulators that allow BF = 1.0 for pipelines typically require a high length-to-diameter ratio (commonly 40:1 or greater). Choosing the “Custom / tracer-study value” option in the baffling dropdown lets you enter a site-specific T10 / HRT ratio measured with a chemical tracer study [2].

Assumptions and limits
  • Use the effective water volume at the actual minimum operating water level rather than the full tank depth, because lower operating levels reduce V and CT.
  • Checking CT at peak design flow is more conservative than checking at low flow, since higher Q shortens HRT and T10.
  • Disinfectant residual decays across a basin, so using the residual measured at the segment outlet is standard regulatory practice.
  • For multiple disinfection segments in series (such as a clearwell followed by a transmission pipeline), calculate each segment separately and sum the resulting credits or CT ratios.
  • Required CT values depend on disinfectant type, water temperature, pH, and target log inactivation (for example, 3-log inactivation means 99.9% pathogen inactivation) from EPA CT tables [1]. The state primacy agency (the state regulator delegated to enforce federal drinking water rules) and the facility's operating permit govern compliance and any tracer-study requirements [2].
  • The calculator is a single offline HTML file with no external dependencies. Inputs are saved in browser localStorage (local storage on your device) until cleared.
