Timing Low-Pressure Air and Manhole Vacuum Tests

June 2026

This calculator computes required hold times and adjusted pressures for two acceptance tests on new gravity sewers (sewer pipes that drain by slope rather than pumps): low-pressure air testing on pipe runs and vacuum testing on concrete manholes. Switch between the Gravity Sewer Air Test and Manhole Vacuum Test tabs, enter the dimensions, and click Set from result to load the hold time into the built-in countdown timer (with Start, Pause, and Reset controls).

The pipe air test follows ASTM F1417 [1] and Uni-Bell Recommended Practice UNI-B-6 [2], based on an allowable air loss rate of 0.0015 cfm (cubic feet per minute) per square foot of internal pipe surface for a 1.0 psig (pounds per square inch gauge, pressure above atmospheric pressure) drop from 3.5 to 2.5 psig. The minimum hold time is T = 0.085 · D · K / Q with K = max(1.0, 0.000419 · D · L), where T is the minimum hold time in seconds, D is nominal pipe diameter in inches, L is test section length in feet, K is a dimensionless length factor (at least 1.0), and Q = 0.0015 cfm/ft2 is the allowable air loss rate. For short sections where L ≤ 2387 / D ft, K = 1.0 and the hold time depends only on diameter (T = 56.67 · D seconds, or 7 min 34 s for an 8-inch pipe). Longer sections scale with D² · L. Choosing the 0.5 psig drop option (3.5 to 3.0 psig) cuts all hold times in half [1][2].

When groundwater stands above the pipe crown (the top of the pipe), external water head (water-column pressure) pushes inward against escaping test air. The calculator adds 0.433 · h_gw psi to the pressurization (4.0 psig), start (3.5 psig), and fail pressures, where h_gw is average groundwater height above the pipe crown in feet. If the adjusted initial pressure exceeds 9 psig, the tool shows a warning because higher air pressure increases plug thrust (outward force on the test plugs), and dewatering (pumping down the groundwater) or an alternate test method is typically used instead.

The manhole tab looks up hold times from the ASTM C1244 vacuum test table [4]. A vacuum of 10 in. Hg (inches of mercury) is drawn on the sealed manhole, and the structure passes if it takes at least the tabulated time to drop to 9 in. Hg. Because a larger barrel volume (the interior shaft volume) holds more air, required times increase with manhole diameter (30 to 72 in.) and depth (8 to 30 ft). Intermediate depths are rounded up to the next 2-foot table row, and depths beyond 30 ft are linearly extrapolated with a warning. Inputs and the active tab are saved in browser localStorage (local storage on your device) and can be cleared with the Clear all inputs button.

Assumptions and limits
  • Standard air-test practice allows at least 2 minutes for compressed air temperature to stabilize with the pipe wall before bleeding back to start pressure and beginning the timer [1][2].
  • In hot weather, wetting the interior of dry pipe (where specifications allow) helps avoid pressure drops caused by warm dry walls cooling or absorbing moisture from test air.
  • Common manhole vacuum leak locations include the frame-to-chimney joint (where the top frame and grade rings meet the manhole cone), flexible rubber pipe boots, and unplugged precast lifting holes [4].
  • ASTM F1417 applies to plastic pipe. Vitrified clay and concrete sewer lines use companion low-pressure air test standards ASTM C828 and ASTM C924 [3]. Where contract specifications include a test table, the contract table governs.
