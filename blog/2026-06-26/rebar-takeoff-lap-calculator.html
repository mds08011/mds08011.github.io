Rebar Weight Takeoff and ACI 318 Lap Splice Lengths

June 2026

This page combines two reinforcing steel (rebar) calculations. The first section is a multi-line weight takeoff (a quantity list for ordering and handling estimates) that sums deformed bar (ribbed steel reinforcing bar) weights across standard US bar sizes #3 through #18, where sizes #3 through #8 correspond to nominal diameters in eighths of an inch (for example, a #4 bar has a 0.500 in nominal diameter). Each row computes W = n × L × wb, where W is weight in pounds (lb), n is the number of bars, L is length per bar in feet (ft), and wb is the ASTM A615 nominal unit weight in pounds per foot (lb/ft, such as 0.668 lb/ft for #4 bars and 1.043 lb/ft for #5 bars) [3]. Totals are shown in pounds, US short tons (2,000 lb), and with a 5% placing allowance.

The second section computes tension development length (ℓd, the embedment length required for concrete to develop a bar's yield strength) and tension lap splice lengths (the overlap needed when splicing two bars side by side in tension) using the ACI 318-14 simplified equations [1]. For Case 1 spacing and cover (clear spacing at least db, clear concrete cover at least db, and code-minimum transverse stirrups or ties, or clear spacing at least 2db with clear cover at least db), the equations are:

#6 and smaller: ℓd = (fy · ψt · ψe / (25 · λ · √f′c)) · db
#7 and larger: ℓd = (fy · ψt · ψe / (20 · λ · √f′c)) · db

Here ℓd is development length in inches (minimum 12 in), fy is bar yield strength in pounds per square inch (psi; 60,000 for Grade 60 or 80,000 for Grade 80), f′c is concrete compressive strength in psi (with √f′c capped at 100 psi), db is nominal bar diameter in inches, ψt is the casting location factor (1.3 for horizontal top bars with more than 12 in of fresh concrete cast below, 1.0 otherwise), ψe is the epoxy coating factor (1.5 for epoxy-coated bars in this simplified tool, 1.0 for uncoated bars, with the product ψt · ψe capped at 1.7), and λ is the concrete weight factor (1.0 for normalweight concrete, 0.75 for lightweight concrete) [1]. Case 2 (tighter spacing or thinner cover) multiplies ℓd by 1.5. A Class B tension lap splice is 1.3 · ℓd and a Class A splice is 1.0 · ℓd (minimum 12 in for both) [1].

Assumptions and limits
  • Most structural drawings default tension lap splices to Class B. Class A is permitted only when the area of steel provided is at least twice the area required by analysis over the entire splice length and no more than half the bars are spliced at one location [1].
  • Under ACI 318, #14 and #18 bars cannot be lap spliced in tension and require mechanical couplers or welded splices, though ℓd still applies for anchorage [1].
  • ACI 318-19 added a reinforcement grade factor ψg = 1.15 for Grade 80 bars (1.0 for Grade 60), which makes Grade 80 laps roughly 53% longer than Grade 60 instead of 33% longer under ACI 318-14 [2]. This calculator uses the ACI 318-14 equations without ψg, so projects governed by ACI 318-19 using Grade 80 steel should multiply the Grade 80 result by 1.15 or follow the project splice schedule.
  • Each lap splice adds one lap length of bar to the takeoff. On long walls or slabs built from standard 60-ft stock bars, lap allowances should be included in the bar lengths. Splice locations, splice stagger (offsetting adjacent splices), and placing tolerances follow the structural drawings and CRSI standard practice [4].
  • Where structural drawings include a lap splice schedule, the drawings govern. Confirm steel grade on the fabricator bar tags before ordering or placing. The tool runs offline in the browser and saves inputs in localStorage (browser-side local storage).
