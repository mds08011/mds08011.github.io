Channel Cross-Section Drawer (Manning's Equation)

July 2026

This calculator computes open-channel flow using Manning's equation and draws the channel cross-section to scale as an inline SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics) diagram with the water area shaded and dimensions labeled. It is a visual companion to the text-only Manning's flow calculator. To use it, choose either a trapezoidal channel (entering bottom width b in ft and side slope Z as horizontal-to-vertical ratio, H:1V) or a circular pipe (entering inside diameter D in inches), enter Manning's roughness n and longitudinal slope S (in percent or ft/ft), and either provide a flow depth y to calculate flow or provide a design flow Q to solve for normal depth (the steady, uniform flow depth).

In US customary units, Manning's equation is V = (1.486 / n) · R^(2/3) · S^(1/2) and Q = V · A [1]. Here V is average velocity (ft/s), n is Manning's roughness coefficient (dimensionless, representing surface resistance), R = A / P is hydraulic radius (ft), A is cross-sectional flow area (ft²), P is wetted perimeter (ft, the length of channel boundary touching water), S is channel slope (ft/ft), and Q is flow rate in cubic feet per second (cfs, also converted to gallons per minute, gpm). For a trapezoid, A = (b + Z · y) · y, P = b + 2 · y · √(1 + Z²), and top width T = b + 2 · Z · y. For a circular pipe with diameter D (ft) and central angle θ = 2 · arccos(1 − 2 · y / D) (radians), A = (D² / 8) · (θ − sin θ), P = D · θ / 2, and T = D · sin(θ / 2) [1].

The tool also calculates the Froude number, Fr = V / √(g · A / T), where g = 32.174 ft/s² is gravitational acceleration [1]. Flow is subcritical (slower and deeper) when Fr < 1, critical near Fr = 1, and supercritical (faster and shallower) when Fr > 1. Because Manning's equation cannot be solved directly for depth in closed form, design-flow mode uses a bisection search (repeatedly halving a depth interval until computed flow matches the target). In a circular pipe under gravity flow, calculated discharge reaches its maximum near a depth-to-diameter ratio d/D = 0.938 (about 94% full) rather than at 100% full, because the last increment of depth near the crown (inside top of the pipe) adds wetted perimeter faster than flow area [1]. The solver stops at d/D = 0.938 and flags higher design flows as exceeding gravity capacity, meaning the pipe would surcharge (run under pressure).

Assumptions and limits
  • Steady, uniform flow: Manning's equation assumes constant discharge, constant slope, and a prismatic channel (unchanging cross-section along its length). Channels with bends, transitions, or downstream backwater require a gradually varied flow profile instead [2].
  • Roughness uncertainty: Concrete and smooth pipe linings have narrow n ranges, while vegetated or natural channels vary with grass height, season, and depth [3].
  • Supercritical flow and freeboard: When Fr > 1, standing waves, hydraulic jumps, and required freeboard (extra wall height above the water surface) need separate evaluation beyond a uniform-flow check [2], [3].
  • Local storage: Inputs are stored locally in the browser via localStorage and can be cleared with the reset button.
