Computing Bypass Pumping Total Dynamic Head with Hazen-Williams

May 2026

Sizing a temporary bypass pump requires estimating the total dynamic head (TDH), which is the total equivalent height of water in feet (ft) the pump must overcome at the design flow rate. TDH equals static head (elevation change) plus pipe friction loss plus minor losses from fittings, valves, and connections. The Bypass Pumping TDH Calculator computes friction loss, flow velocity, TDH in feet and pounds per square inch (psi), and a five-point system curve for single or parallel discharge lines.

Friction loss is calculated using the US customary Hazen-Williams empirical formula [1]: h_f = 0.2083 × (100 / C)^1.85 × Q^1.85 / d^4.8655, where h_f is friction slope in feet of head per 100 ft of pipe, C is the dimensionless Hazen-Williams roughness coefficient (higher values indicate smoother pipe, such as 150 for new high-density polyethylene, HDPE, or 140 for rubber lay-flat hose), Q is flow per individual discharge line in gallons per minute (GPM), and d is the actual pipe inside diameter in inches (in). Total pipe friction loss is h_f × L / 100 for line length L in feet, and average velocity in feet per second (ft/s) is V = 0.4085 × Q / d². Because friction varies inversely with d^4.8655, using nominal pipe size instead of actual inside diameter can introduce large errors. For Iron Pipe Size (IPS) HDPE pipe, nominal size corresponds to the outside diameter, so wall thickness set by the Dimension Ratio (DR, outside diameter divided by wall thickness, such as DR 11 or DR 17) reduces the internal bore [2]. The calculator includes an HDPE inside-diameter reference table and preset C values.

Total dynamic head sums suction lift (vertical height from the water surface in the manhole or wet well up to the pump, 0 ft for submersible or flooded-suction pumps), discharge static head (discharge elevation minus pump elevation, negative for downhill discharge), pipe friction loss, and a user-entered percentage minor-loss allowance (typically 10% to 15%). Head converts to pressure via psi = TDH × 0.4331. Below the design-point summary, the tool prints a five-point system-curve table recomputing velocity and TDH at 50%, 75%, 100%, 125%, and 150% of design flow. Plotting those system-curve points against a pump manufacturer's published pump curve (head versus flow) gives the operating point where the two curves intersect.

The calculator is a single offline HTML page with no external dependencies, and entered values are stored locally on your device using browser localStorage.

Assumptions and limits
  • Low velocity: in sewage bypass lines, velocities below about 2 ft/s allow solids, grease, and rags to settle and later flush out in concentrated slugs (many specifications require 2.5 to 3.5 ft/s). Check velocity at average daily flow as well as peak design flow.
  • High velocity: above about 8 to 10 ft/s, friction loss climbs steeply, restrained fittings carry higher thrust loads, and flexible discharge hose can whip at the outlet. Using a larger pipe diameter or an additional parallel line reduces both velocity and head loss.
  • Suction lift and siphoning: suction lift above about 20 ft approaches the practical priming limit of surface-mounted trash pumps, requiring a net positive suction head check or a submersible pump. When negative static head on a downhill run exceeds friction loss, the line may siphon and draw in air.
  • Formula scope: the Hazen-Williams equation applies to full-pipe, turbulent flow of clean water at ordinary temperatures [1]. It does not model heavy sludge, high solids concentrations, or air entrainment (trapped air bubbles). Always verify final pump sizing against the specific pump manufacturer's published curve and the approved bypass plan.
