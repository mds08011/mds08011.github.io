Calculating Net Positive Suction Head Available (NPSHa)

June 2026

This calculator computes Net Positive Suction Head Available (NPSHa) at a pump inlet and compares it against Net Positive Suction Head Required (NPSHr), the minimum suction head a pump needs at its operating flow rate. Head is pressure expressed as the height of an equivalent column of water (in feet). If pressure at the pump inlet drops to the liquid's vapor pressure, the water boils into vapor bubbles that collapse inside the pump. That process, called cavitation, can pit the rotating impeller and reduce flow and head [2].

The tool sums four suction terms, all in feet of water [2]:

NPSHa = h_atm ± h_static − h_vp − h_friction  (ft of water)

Here h_atm is local atmospheric pressure head (ft), computed from site elevation elev (ft above sea level) using the U.S. Standard Atmosphere formula P_atm = 14.696 × (1 − 6.8754×10⁻⁶ × elev)^5.2559 in pounds per square inch absolute (psia, pressure relative to a full vacuum) [3] and multiplied by 2.31 ft/psi (the conversion for clean water with specific gravity near 1). The static term h_static is the vertical distance (ft) between the water surface and the pump centerline, added for flooded suction (water surface above the pump) and subtracted for suction lift (water surface below the pump). The vapor pressure term h_vp (ft) is linearly interpolated from a 32°F to 212°F water vapor pressure table (in psia) and multiplied by 2.31 ft/psi. The friction term h_friction (ft) is the head loss through the suction pipe, fittings, valves, and strainer.

Water temperature has a large effect on h_vp: vapor pressure is 0.26 psia (about 0.6 ft of water) at 60°F, rises to 2.22 psia (about 5.1 ft) at 130°F, and reaches 7.51 psia (about 17.3 ft) at 180°F. When an optional NPSHr value (from the manufacturer's certified pump curve at the actual operating flow) and a required safety margin are entered, the calculator reports whether NPSHa meets NPSHr plus the margin. Hydraulic Institute guideline ANSI/HI 9.6.1 recommends maintaining an explicit margin above NPSHr because published NPSHr typically marks a 3% head drop where cavitation is already underway [1].

Assumptions and limits
  • The vapor pressure table and 2.31 ft/psi conversion assume clean water with specific gravity close to 1. Other liquids or temperatures outside 32°F to 212°F need fluid-specific properties.
  • Read NPSHr from the manufacturer's certified pump curve at the actual operating flow rate, not just the rated design point. NPSHr rises steeply at high flow rates, so a pump running against low system head (total elevation lift plus pipe friction) can run out of margin unless flow is reduced, such as by partially closing a discharge valve.
  • Evaluate suction conditions at the lowest expected water level and include strainer or foot-valve losses, especially when a suction screen can partially plug.
  • An adequate NPSHa margin does not guarantee good intake hydraulics. Minimum water submergence above the suction inlet to prevent air-drawing vortices is a separate check under Hydraulic Institute intake standards.
