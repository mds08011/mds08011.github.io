Culvert Headwater Check (FHWA HDS-5)

June 2026

This calculator performs a preliminary headwater check for a single circular culvert (a pipe passing water under a road or embankment) using the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) Hydraulic Design Series No. 5 (HDS-5) hand method [1]. A culvert operates under either inlet control (the entrance geometry limits capacity) or outlet control (barrel friction, exit losses, and downstream water depth limit capacity). The calculator computes the headwater depth (HW, the upstream water depth above the inlet invert, in ft) for both conditions and reports the larger value as the governing headwater.

Inlet control uses the HDS-5 Appendix A regression equations in US customary units, based on the discharge parameter Q / (A · D^0.5), where Q is flow rate in cubic feet per second (cfs), A is the full pipe cross-sectional area in square feet (ft²), and D is inside pipe diameter in feet (ft) [1]. For unsubmerged inlets (Q / (A · D^0.5) ≤ 3.5), HW / D = H_c / D + K · [Q / (A · D^0.5)]^M − 0.5 · S, where H_c = d_c + V_c² / (2g) is critical specific energy (ft), d_c is critical depth (ft), V_c is velocity at critical depth (ft/s), g = 32.174 ft/s² is gravitational acceleration, S is barrel slope (ft/ft), and K and M are dimensionless inlet coefficients. For submerged inlets (Q / (A · D^0.5) ≥ 4.0, where the entrance acts as an orifice), HW / D = c · [Q / (A · D^0.5)]² + Y − 0.5 · S, where c and Y are dimensionless inlet coefficients. Between 3.5 and 4.0, the calculator interpolates linearly. Six concrete and corrugated metal pipe (CMP) entrance types are included. For mitered inlets (pipe ends cut flush with the embankment slope), the slope term is +0.7 · S instead of −0.5 · S [1].

Outlet control assumes the barrel flows full and computes total head loss in US customary units as H = [1 + K_e + 29 · n² · L / R^(4/3)] · V² / (2g), where H is total head loss (ft), K_e is the dimensionless entrance loss coefficient, n is Manning's roughness coefficient (dimensionless), L is barrel length (ft), R = D / 4 is full-pipe hydraulic radius (ft), V = Q / A is full-flow velocity (ft/s), and 29 is the US customary unit factor (2g / 1.486²) [1]. Outlet-control headwater is HW = H + h_o − S · L, where h_o = max(TW, (d_c + D) / 2) approximates the downstream control depth (ft) and TW is tailwater depth above the outlet invert (ft). Critical depth (d_c, the depth where Froude number equals 1) is solved by bisection (repeatedly halving a depth bracket until convergence) on Q² · T / (g · A_w³) = 1, where T is water-surface top width (ft) and A_w is wetted flow area (ft²) at depth d_c.

Assumptions and limits
  • Design standards often limit the headwater-to-diameter ratio (HW/D) to 1.2 to 1.5 at the design storm (the target runoff event), and the tool flags values above 1.5. Roadway overtopping checks compare governing HW directly to the road profile elevation.
  • When inlet control governs by a large margin, an improved entrance (such as a beveled or grooved end with a headwall, a retaining wall built around the pipe entrance) can increase capacity without enlarging the pipe diameter [1].
  • Full-flow outlet velocities above about 10 ft/s typically require outlet scour protection such as riprap (graded rock armor) or an energy dissipator sized per FHWA Hydraulic Engineering Circular No. 14 (HEC-14) [3].
  • The equations assume an unobstructed barrel. Sediment buildup, debris blockage, and maintenance access must be evaluated separately.
  • This is a preliminary hand check for a single circular barrel assuming full flow under outlet control. For partially full outlet-control profiles, box culverts, multiple barrels, or final design analysis, use FHWA's HY-8 software [2].

The calculator runs in the browser and saves inputs in browser local storage (localStorage, a built-in browser store on the current device) until cleared.

The tool: Culvert Headwater Quick-Check (HDS-5).
