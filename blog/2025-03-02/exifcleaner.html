Removing photo GPS data with ExifCleaner

2025.03

Most of the time I want GPS coordinates saved on my photos for personal archiving and travel logs. When I'm posting a photo publicly, though, I don't want my location attached to the file. ExifCleaner1 is the desktop app I use for stripping that out.

What ExifCleaner does

Photos store hidden metadata called EXIF data (camera model, date taken, and often exact GPS coordinates). ExifCleaner is a free, open-source program for Windows, macOS, and Linux that removes that metadata. Under the hood it runs ExifTool (a command-line utility), wrapped in a drag-and-drop window so you don't have to touch a terminal.

How to use it

Download the installer for your OS from the ExifCleaner site1, open the app, and drag your photos onto the window. Everything runs locally on your computer, so nothing is uploaded to a server. One thing to watch out for: ExifCleaner modifies files in place, so make a copy of your photo first if you want to keep the GPS data on your personal original.

The source code is on GitHub.2
