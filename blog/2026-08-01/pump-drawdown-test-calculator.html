Pump Station Drawdown Test Calculator

August 2026

A wet-well drawdown test estimates pump discharge (outlet flow rate) at a lift station (a wastewater or stormwater pumping station) when no discharge flowmeter is installed [3]. The test uses the wet well (the sump feeding the pumps) as a calibrated volume by timing water-level changes read from the station's level transducer, SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition) display, staff gauge, or measuring tape while operating the pumps under controlled test procedures. This calculator computes inflow, net drawdown rate, and corrected pump capacity, and plots a two-segment water-level trace.

For a wet well with constant horizontal plan area A (ft²), a level change d (ft) over an elapsed time t (min) corresponds to a volumetric flow rate Q = 7.4805 · A · d / t, where Q is flow rate in gallons per minute (gpm) and 7.4805 is the conversion factor in gallons per cubic foot (gal/ft³) [3]. The tool accepts plan area directly or computes it from rectangular length and width or circular diameter (in ft), and accepts level changes in inches or feet and times in seconds or minutes. Total pump capacity is also converted to cubic feet per second (cfs).

When incoming flow (influent) is not isolated during the test, water continues entering the wet well while the pump runs, so the water surface falls at the net rate Qnet = Qpump − Qin [3]. Measuring only the level drop gives Qnet and understates pump capacity. To correct for inflow, the test first times the water-level rise with all pumps off to find Qin (gpm), then times the level drop with the pump running to find Qnet (gpm), and adds them: Qpump = Qnet + Qin. If the influent line is blocked or diverted, checking "Influent isolated" sets Qin = 0.

Assumptions and limits
  • Constant plan area: The volume equation assumes vertical, uniform walls over the measured band. Sloped benching, concrete fillets (angled corners near the floor), and the influent pipe invert (bottom inside elevation) reduce or change the horizontal area if included in the measurement zone [1].
  • Control setpoints and inflow stability: The test band should stay between the automatic pump-off and pump-on setpoints (float or transducer trigger levels) so controls do not cycle the pump mid-test, and inflow is assumed roughly steady between the rise and drawdown intervals [3].
  • Repeated measurements: Timing errors have a larger effect on short intervals, especially on small pump-off rises. Repeating the cycle several times and averaging reduces measurement scatter [2].
  • Operating head vs. rated head: Compare the measured flow against the pump curve at the actual operating head during the test rather than only the rated design point, because higher system head lowers pump discharge [2]. You can check expected flow with the pump system-curve plotter and check pump cycle frequency during commissioning (initial startup testing) with the wet-well cycle-time calculator.
  • Local storage: Inputs are stored locally in the browser via localStorage and can be cleared with the reset button.
