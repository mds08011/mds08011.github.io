Estimating Annular and Abandonment Grout Volumes

May 2026

Overview

This calculator estimates material order volumes for grouting an annular space (the ring-shaped gap between an outer protective casing pipe and an inner carrier pipe carrying water or sewer flow), filling an abandoned pipe, or filling an abandoned rectangular structure. It also includes a direct-volume mode for converting a known volume in cubic feet (cf) into ordering units.

Formulas

Neat geometric volume in cubic feet is calculated for three shapes:

  • Annular space: V = (π / 4) × (D_casing² − D_carrier²) / 144 × L, where V is volume in cubic feet (cf), D_casing is the casing inside diameter in inches, D_carrier is the carrier pipe outside diameter in inches, 144 converts square inches to square feet, and L is the casing length in feet.
  • Pipe abandonment (full bore): V = (π / 4) × D_ID² / 144 × L, where D_ID is the abandoned pipe inside diameter in inches and L is the pipe length in feet.
  • Rectangular structure: V = L × W × depth, where L is inside length, W is inside width, and depth is fill depth, all in feet.

The tool adds a user-chosen overage percentage and converts the order volume into cubic yards (CY, at 27 cf per CY), gallons (at 7.48 gallons per cf), and dry sacks (using a user-entered sack yield in cubic feet per sack). Ready-mix controlled low-strength material (CLSM, a flowable cementitious backfill covered in ACI 229R [1]) is usually ordered in cubic yards, while grout pumps and mixers are often sized in gallons or cubic feet. In annular mode, the tool also reports radial clearance between the carrier pipe and the casing.

Assumptions and limits
  • Volume calculations are for ordering materials. Standard placement practice is to seal the ends with bulkheads (temporary plugs), pump from the low end, and confirm fill by observing grout return at a high-end vent. Actual volume taken can differ from neat geometry if grout enters voids outside the casing or flows into connected laterals (side branch pipes) and structures.
  • An empty carrier pipe, especially lightweight plastic pipe such as high-density polyethylene (HDPE), experiences upward buoyancy force in fluid grout and can float against the casing crown (the top inside wall of the casing). Standard precautions include filling the carrier pipe with water before grouting, placing grout in stages, using restrained spacers (supports that lock the carrier pipe in place), or using cellular (foamed) grout made with preformed foam to reduce density.
  • Contract specifications govern the grout or CLSM mix, placement pressure limits, and verification requirements. Sack yields and cellular grout mix designs vary by product and must be taken from the manufacturer's product data sheet or supplier mix design.

The calculator runs offline in a single HTML file using plain JavaScript with no external libraries, and saves your inputs in your browser's local storage (localStorage).
