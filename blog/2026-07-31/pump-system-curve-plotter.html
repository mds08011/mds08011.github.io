Pump and System Curve Plotter

July 2026

A centrifugal pump operates at the flow rate and head (energy per unit weight of water, expressed in feet of water column) where the pump's head-capacity curve intersects the piping system's head-loss curve [2]. This calculator plots both curves on the same axes, finds their intersection (the operating point), and supports combining up to four identical pumps in parallel.

To enter the pump curve, read four to six pairs of flow rate Q in gallons per minute (gpm) and head H in feet (ft) from the manufacturer's chart, including shutoff (zero flow) and a point near the right end of the curve. The tool fits a least-squares quadratic curve (minimizing squared error to the entered points): Hpump = a + b · Q + c · Q², where a is fitted shutoff head (ft) and b and c are curve coefficients. For the piping system, enter the static head Hstatic (ft, the vertical elevation lift plus any discharge pressure head independent of flow) and either a design point (a flow Qdesign in gpm and its friction head loss Hf in ft) or a friction coefficient K directly. The system curve is Hsys = Hstatic + K · Qn, where K = Hf / Qdesignn (ft / gpmn) and n is the friction exponent (2.0 for Darcy-Weisbach head loss or 1.85 for Hazen-Williams head loss) [3]. When shutoff head exceeds Hstatic, the tool uses bisection (repeatedly halving a flow bracket) to find the flow where Hpump = Hsys.

When N identical pumps operate in parallel (discharging into the same header), their flows add at equal head, so the combined curve evaluates the single-pump quadratic at Q / N [2]. Because pipe friction increases roughly with the square of flow, running a second pump moves the operating point to a higher head on the system curve, which gives less than double the single-pump flow. In the example shown in Figure 1, one pump delivers about 1,650 gpm at 76 ft, while two identical pumps in parallel deliver about 2,100 gpm (a 27% increase).

Notes and limits
  • Speed and impeller changes: A manufacturer's curve applies to one rotational speed and impeller (rotating wheel) diameter. If a VFD (variable frequency drive) or trimmed impeller changes the curve, scale the points with the pump affinity laws calculator before plotting.
  • System curve range: Friction losses change with pipe age, valve throttling, and varying tank levels. Checking both a low-friction (new pipe) and high-friction (aged pipe) case shows the range of operating points.
  • Operating region and suction margin: Check that the operating point stays near the pump's BEP (best efficiency point). Running far right of BEP can increase the risk of cavitation (vapor bubble collapse) and motor overload, while running far left can cause internal recirculation and vibration [1]. Check suction pressure separately with the NPSH (net positive suction head) available calculator.
  • Friction head input: The total dynamic head (TDH) calculator can be used to estimate static head and friction head loss at a design flow.
  • Local storage: Inputs are stored locally in the browser via localStorage and can be cleared with the reset button.
