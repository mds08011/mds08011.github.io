VFD Hand and Auto Rotation Checklist

July 2026

A variable frequency drive (VFD) is an electronic motor controller that regulates a three-phase motor's speed and direction. Most VFD-driven equipment can be run in two modes: Hand mode (local manual control at the drive keypad or panel) and Auto mode (remote automatic control from a programmable logic controller, or PLC, or a supervisory control and data acquisition, or SCADA, system). Unlike an across-the-line motor wired directly to a starter, a VFD can receive an inverted direction setting or speed reference in one mode while running forward in the other. Swapping two power leads after testing only Hand mode can reverse Auto mode into the wrong direction.

This checklist tracks observed motor rotation per equipment ID across both modes. For each piece of equipment, you select Forward, Reverse, or Untested for Hand and for Auto, and the page evaluates the combination using a simple rule based on three-phase motor wiring [1]. Because the same three power leads feed the motor in both Hand and Auto, physical wiring affects both modes identically:

  • Hand = Forward, Auto = Forward: Verified.
  • Hand = Reverse, Auto = Reverse: Physical wiring fault. Swap any two of the three power leads (such as L1 and L2) at the motor control center (MCC, the electrical panel feeding the motor) and retest both modes [1].
  • Hand = Reverse, Auto = Forward or Hand = Forward, Auto = Reverse: Software or control conflict. Do not swap physical power leads. Check the drive's direction parameter, the PLC or SCADA speed-reference sign, or the control terminal wiring.
  • Hand = Untested or Auto = Untested: Testing incomplete. No verdict is shown until both modes are recorded.

Entries are saved automatically in the browser's localStorage (local device storage that persists across page reloads) and can be exported as a comma-separated values (CSV) spreadsheet file for commissioning (equipment startup) records. The tool runs offline in the browser with no external scripts.

Notes and limits
  • Check the motor and driven-equipment operation and maintenance (O&M) manual before bump-testing (briefly jogging the motor at low speed to observe rotation). Positive-displacement machines such as screw pumps and some gearboxes can be damaged if rotated backward even briefly and may need to be uncoupled first.
  • Confirm rotation against the direction arrow cast or stamped on the equipment casing. A centrifugal machine (such as a centrifugal pump or blower) running backward can still deliver roughly half its rated flow and head (pressure expressed as fluid column height) while sounding close to normal.
  • After any drive parameter adjustment or power-lead swap, retest both Hand and Auto modes to verify both match the casing arrow.
  • The drive manufacturer's parameter manual governs software direction settings, and the equipment O&M manual governs mechanical rotation limits.
