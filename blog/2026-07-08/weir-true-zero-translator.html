Converting Ultrasonic Level Readings to Weir Flow

July 2026

An ultrasonic level element (LE) mounted over an open-channel weir measures water surface position in feet, referenced to its own sensor datum (zero point) rather than the weir crest (the bottom edge of the notch where water begins to spill). To convert a raw reading into discharge, you first need the true zero: the sensor reading when the water surface sits exactly level with the crest. Subtracting between the live reading and the true zero gives the head over the weir, which feeds a standard sharp-crested (thin-plate) weir equation.

Because some overhead ultrasonic sensors output water level (reading increases as water rises) while others output distance down from the sensor face to the water (reading decreases as water rises), the calculator includes a direction toggle:

  • Level mode: H = R_live − R_zero
  • Distance-down mode: H = R_zero − R_live

Here H is head above the weir crest in feet (ft), R_live is the current sensor reading in ft, and R_zero is the true-zero crest reading in ft. When H ≤ 0, the water surface is at or below the crest and flow is reported as zero.

From H, the calculator computes discharge Q in cubic feet per second (cfs) using standard sharp-crested weir equations [1][2]:

  • 90° V-notch weir: Q = 2.5 · H^2.5 (field form, while the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, or USBR, also gives 2.49 · H^2.48, which agrees within about 1% over normal heads) [1]
  • 60° V-notch weir: Q = 1.443 · H^2.5 (from 2.5 · tan(30°) · H^2.5) [1]
  • Suppressed rectangular weir (full channel width, no side contractions): Q = 3.33 · L · H^1.5, where L is the weir crest width in ft [1]

The tool converts Q from cfs to gallons per minute (GPM) and million gallons per day (MGD) using 1 cfs = 448.831 GPM = 0.646317 MGD, and displays H in feet and inches. Inputs are saved in the browser's localStorage (local device storage) so the true-zero datum persists between page reloads.

Assumptions and limits
  • Free discharge is required: air must circulate beneath the nappe (the sheet of water spilling over the crest), with no downstream submergence backing water up against the weir [1][2].
  • The calculator displays a general warning when H < 0.2 ft (where surface tension causes the nappe to cling to the plate) or H > 2.0 ft. Under USBR Chapter 7 [1] and ISO 1438 [2], the rated upper head limit for a rectangular weir depends on crest width (commonly H ≤ L / 3) and approach-channel geometry rather than a fixed 2.0 ft ceiling.
  • The rectangular equation assumes a suppressed weir. If the notch is narrower than the channel (a contracted rectangular weir), the effective crest width is reduced by L − 0.2 · H. For Cipolletti (trapezoidal) weirs, contracted rectangular weirs, or submerged orifices (underwater openings), use the Weir & Orifice Flow Calculator.
  • Ultrasonic sensors average over an acoustic beam footprint and can fluctuate if foam, steam, or surface waves are present. Check stable readings rather than a single transient sample.
  • This calculator is intended for quick verification checks rather than custody-transfer or billing measurement.
