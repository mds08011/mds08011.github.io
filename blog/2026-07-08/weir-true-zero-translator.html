Translating a Raw Ultrasonic Reading into Weir Flow

July 2026

The Problem

An ultrasonic level element over a diversion weir reports a number in feet. The question on site is GPM. Between those two is a datum problem and a hydraulics problem: the instrument has no idea where the weir crest is, and the person reading it doesn't have Q = 2.5·H^2.5 memorized. The missing ingredient is one calibration fact — the true zero, the instrument's reading at the moment the water surface sits exactly at the crest. Capture that once and every future reading is a subtraction away from head, and head is a power law away from flow.

The Approach

Enter the true zero, the weir type, and the live reading; the tool does the rest and prints GPM in numbers you can read from arm's length in sunlight:

H = live − true zero  →  Q = 2.5·H^2.5 (90° V-notch)  or  1.443·H^2.5 (60°)  or  3.33·L·H^1.5 (rectangular, suppressed)

with output in GPM, MGD, and cfs. If H comes out at or below zero, the tool says “water below crest — no flow” instead of printing a negative flow, and it warns outside the 0.2–2.0 ft band where sharp-crested formulas are actually rated [1].

The direction toggle

One wrinkle the simple subtraction misses: many ultrasonic LEs mounted above the channel report distance down to the water — the reading falls as the water rises. Subtract in the wrong direction and you get confidently wrong flow. The tool has a two-way toggle: level mode computes live − true zero, distance mode computes true zero − live. Either way, the true zero is captured the same way — note the reading when the water sits at the crest.

Accuracy fine print
  • The weir must run free — air under the nappe, no submergence from downstream.
  • Ultrasonics average over a footprint and dislike foam and standing waves; if the raw reading bounces, read the trend, not one sample.
  • This is a startup verification device, not a billing meter. The fuller device set (Cipolletti, contracted rectangular, submerged orifice) lives in the Weir & Orifice Flow Calculator.

Single file, vanilla JS, inputs persisted in localStorage [2].
