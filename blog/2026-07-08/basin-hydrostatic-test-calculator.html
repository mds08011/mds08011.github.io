Water-Tightness Testing Concrete Basins per ACI 350.1

July 2026

This calculator checks volumetric liquid loss during a hydrostatic (water-tightness) test of a concrete basin, tank, or channel using the American Concrete Institute (ACI) 350.1 method [1]. You enter the basin shape and dimensions (or water surface area and volume directly), the measured water surface drop, the test duration, the evaporation and rainfall correction, and the specified allowable loss rate (commonly 0.05% or 0.10% of test volume per 24 hours). The calculator reports the corrected leakage drop, volume lost, 24-hour loss rate, pass/fail status, and the pre-test allowable drop for the planned duration.

Water surface elevation changes from evaporation and rainfall must be removed before computing leakage. With a floating evaporation pan (an open calibrated pan floated in the basin so it experiences the same evaporation and rainfall as the basin surface), the corrected leakage drop is Δh = drop_basin − Δh_pan, where Δh is the net leakage drop (in), drop_basin is the measured basin water surface drop (in), and Δh_pan is the pan water surface change (in, positive for a drop and negative for a rise). When evaporation and rainfall are estimated separately, the corrected drop is Δh = drop_basin − E + P, where E is evaporation (in) and P is rainfall (in) over the test period.

The corrected drop converts to leakage volume by V_lost = (Δh / 12) × A × 7.4805, where V_lost is leakage volume (gal), A is water surface area at the test water level (ft2, equal to length times width for a rectangle or π × D2 / 4 for a circle of inside diameter D in ft), 12 converts inches to feet, and 7.4805 converts cubic feet to US gallons. The 24-hour loss rate is L_24 = (V_lost / V_test) × (24 / t_hr) × 100, where L_24 is percent of test volume lost per 24 hours, V_test is the initial water volume at test level (gal), and t_hr is the test duration (hr). Solving in reverse gives the pre-test allowable drop: Δh_allow = (allow% / 100) × (V_test / (A × 7.4805)) × 12 × (t_hr / 24), where Δh_allow is the maximum allowable corrected drop (in) and allow% is the specified allowable loss (% of volume per 24 hr).

Two parts of the ACI 350.1 procedure directly affect whether a test measurement is valid [1]. First, dry concrete absorbs water after filling, so ACI 350.1 specifies a stabilization period (commonly 3 days for unlined concrete) before the measured test period begins. Second, with a hook gauge (a precision depth gauge with a vernier scale) or fixed staff, practical reading resolution is around 1/16 in (0.0625 in). When the allowable drop over 24 hours is very small, ACI 350.1 allows extending the test duration so the allowable drop can be resolved reliably, and the calculator flags allowable drops under 0.25 in.

Notes and limits
  • Measure water levels from fixed reference points at multiple locations and at the same time of day to reduce error from wind setup and thermal expansion of the water.
  • Verify isolation valves (such as plug valves) and temporary bulkheads (watertight barriers blocking pipe openings) before testing. Any leakage past an isolation device adds to the measured basin loss.
  • Exterior wall inspection is separate from the volumetric calculation. Project specifications typically distinguish acceptable surface dampness from unacceptable flowing water regardless of the calculated loss rate [1].
  • Contract specifications govern the exact acceptance criterion, stabilization period, test duration, and retest procedure. For circular prestressed concrete tanks (cylindrical tanks compressed with tensioned wire or strands), American Water Works Association (AWWA) standards D110 and D115 specify separate tightness-testing rules [2].
  • The tool is a single HTML page written in plain JavaScript that runs offline and stores inputs in browser localStorage (local storage in the browser on your device).
