Coordinate Converter for Decimal Degrees, DMS, UTM, and California State Plane

May 2026

The Coordinate Converter converts a single point locally in the browser among four formats: decimal degrees (DD, signed latitude and longitude), degrees-minutes-seconds (DMS, with separate degree, minute, second, and hemisphere fields), Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM, a metric grid covering California in zones 10 and 11 split at 120 degrees west), and all six zones of the California State Plane Coordinate System (SPCS) on the North American Datum of 1983 (NAD83) in US survey feet [2][3]. Pick an input format, enter the coordinates, choose the California State Plane output zone, and copy any of the converted lines.

All map projection equations follow Snyder's Map Projections: A Working Manual on the Geodetic Reference System 1980 (GRS80) reference ellipsoid (semi-major axis a = 6,378,137 m, inverse flattening 1/f = 298.257222101) [1]. California SPCS uses the two-standard-parallel Lambert Conformal Conic projection (where the projection cone touches the ellipsoid at two standard parallels, with false easting 2,000,000 m and false northing 500,000 m added to keep coordinates positive) [1][2]. UTM uses the Transverse Mercator projection (central meridian scale factor k₀ = 0.9996, 6-degree zone width, and 500,000 m false easting) [1]. Angle and unit conversions use DD = ±(D + M/60 + S/3600) (with degrees D, arcminutes M, arcseconds S, positive for N/E and negative for S/W) and the exact US survey foot definition ft_US = m × (3937 / 1200), where m is meters and ft_US is US survey feet. The US survey foot (1200 / 3937 m) differs from the international foot (0.3048 m) by about 2 parts per million (ppm), which shifts State Plane coordinates of several million feet by multiple feet. California NAD83 zones in US survey feet correspond to European Petroleum Survey Group (EPSG) codes 2225 through 2230, while the meter versions are EPSG 26941 through 26946 [3].

Because projection math is difficult to check by eye, any implementation should be tested against known geometric properties (such as verifying that a zone origin maps to its defined false easting and northing, that forward-then-inverse round-trips return the starting point, and that the grid scale factor equals 1.0 along a standard parallel) as well as against a published National Geodetic Survey (NGS) control-point datasheet in the project zone [2]. The page runs offline as a single HTML file with no external libraries, and entered inputs are stored locally on your device in browser localStorage.

Assumptions and limits
  • Datum differences (NAD83, WGS84, NAD27): the calculator works strictly on NAD83/GRS80 and does not shift datums. Consumer Global Positioning System (GPS) receivers and web maps such as OpenStreetMap use World Geodetic System 1984 (WGS84), which differs from NAD83 in California by roughly 1 to 2 meters (small enough to ignore for locating a feature on aerial imagery, but unacceptable for survey control or boundary work). Different NAD83 realizations (such as HARN, CORS96, and NAD83(2011)) also differ at the centimeter to decimeter level.
  • Legacy California Zone 7 (NAD27): under the older North American Datum of 1927 (NAD27), California had seven zones, with Zone 7 covering Los Angeles County. Under NAD83, Zone 7 was merged into Zone 5. Drawings labeled Zone 7 use NAD27, which is roughly 90 meters off NAD83 and requires North American Datum Conversion (NADCON) grid-shift transformations in Geographic Information System (GIS) software such as QGIS, PROJ, or NGS online tools [2].
  • Grid distance vs. ground distance: State Plane coordinates measure distance on the map projection grid rather than true horizontal distance on the ground. Precise survey layouts and long lines require applying the combined scale factor (grid scale factor times elevation factor).
  • Sign and zone warnings: west longitudes in California are negative in decimal degrees. The calculator displays a warning for positive longitudes, points outside California, or points far outside the selected State Plane zone's latitude band.
