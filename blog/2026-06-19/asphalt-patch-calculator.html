Asphalt Patch Tonnage for Trench Restoration

June 2026

This calculator converts pavement patch dimensions into order quantities of HMA (hot-mix asphalt) in short tons and truckloads. Under Patch Geometry, select Trench T-patch (a two-layer trench repair where the top layer overhangs the trench edges like the letter T) or Simple patch (a single rectangular layer). For a T-patch, enter patch length, trench width, surface cutback width on each side (the extra strip of existing pavement removed beyond the saw cut so the top layer rests on undisturbed ground), base course depth (the lower structural asphalt layer placed inside the trench width), and surface course depth (the top wearing layer). Under Ordering, set compacted density (default 145 pcf, pounds per cubic foot), waste factor (default 10%), and truck capacity (default 15 tons).

For each course, neat tonnage is T = (A × d / 12) × γ / 2000, where T is neat HMA weight in short tons (2,000 lb per ton), A is course area in square feet (sf), d is compacted lift thickness in inches (in.), and γ is compacted asphalt density in pcf. For a T-patch, the base area is A_base = L × W_trench and the surface area (also reported as pay area in sf and square yards, sy) is A_surf = L × (W_trench + 2 × c), where L is patch length in feet, W_trench is trench width in feet, and c is the cutback width on each side in feet. Total order tonnage is T_order = (T_base + T_surf) × (1 + w / 100), where w is the waste factor in percent, and truckloads are N = ceil(T_order / C_truck) for truck capacity C_truck in tons. As a cross-check, at 145 pcf one inch of compacted asphalt weighs 12.1 lb/sf (109 lb/sy, close to the common 110 lb per square yard per inch rule of thumb), so one short ton covers about 165.5 sf (18.4 sy) at 1 inch of compacted depth [1].

Inputs are saved in browser localStorage (local storage on your device) and can be reset with Clear all inputs. The calculator flags orders smaller than one truckload as a reminder to check plant minimums. Final patch dimensions, cutback widths, and mix types are governed by the agency encroachment permit (the permit authorizing work in the public right-of-way) and standard trench restoration details, and actual compacted density comes from the approved mix design (the laboratory-approved asphalt mix recipe).

Assumptions and limits
  • Asphalt plants often require a minimum order weight (for example, 10 tons). Ordering 10 tons for a 6-ton patch produces 40% excess mix regardless of placement accuracy, so grouping nearby patches into one paving run reduces leftover material.
  • HMA loses workability as it cools, so long haul distances, cold ambient temperatures, or hand placement around utility castings can increase waste [1].
  • Where permitted by the agency, milling (grinding off the top pavement layer) the cutback instead of making a second full-depth saw cut can provide a stepped joint and clean surface for the tack coat (the asphalt emulsion sprayed to bond new mix to existing pavement) [1].
  • Check whether the encroachment permit measures restoration by surface area (sy) or by plant tonnage tickets (scale weight slips for delivered loads), and keep delivery tickets for project records.
