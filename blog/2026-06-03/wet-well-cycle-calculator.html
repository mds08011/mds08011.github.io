Calculating Wet Well Cycle Time and Pump Starts per Hour

June 2026

This calculator checks pump cycle time and starts per hour for a wastewater lift station (a pumping station that lifts sewage to a higher elevation). A lift station collects incoming flow in a storage chamber called a wet well, and a pump cycles on and off between high and low water levels set by float switches or a level transducer (a pressure or ultrasonic depth sensor). Starting an electric motor directly across the line (at full voltage) generates heat in the motor windings, so motor manufacturers limit how many starts per hour are allowed. Too many starts, known as short-cycling, can overheat the motor.

The tool computes effective volume V (gallons, gal) between the pump-on and pump-off levels from rectangular dimensions (length and width in feet), a circular inside diameter (feet), or a direct volume entry, converting cubic feet at 7.48 gal/ft³. For a single constant-speed pump with discharge capacity Q_p (gallons per minute, gpm) and steady incoming flow Q_i (gpm), one full cycle is fill time plus pump-down time [1]:

t_cycle = V / Q_i + V / (Q_p − Q_i)  (minutes)

Starts per hour for the station equal 60 / t_cycle. Cycle time reaches its shortest value when inflow equals half of pump capacity (Q_i = Q_p / 2). Substituting Q_p / 2 gives t_min = 4·V / Q_p (minutes), so the worst-case station start rate is 60 / t_min = 15·Q_p / V starts per hour. Solving for the minimum effective volume needed to stay within an allowable N starts per hour per pump across n alternating pumps gives [1]:

V_min = 15·Q_p / (N·n)  (gal)

In a duplex station with alternation (n = 2), two pumps take turns as the lead pump, so each pump sees half of the station starts. The calculator reports effective volume, worst-case cycle time and starts per hour (for the station and per pump), cycle times at an optional entered inflow, and the minimum required volume. It warns if entered inflow meets or exceeds pump capacity, or if worst-case starts per pump exceed the entered limit.

Assumptions and limits
  • This model assumes a single lead pump cycling at constant flow between fixed on and off levels. Stations with variable frequency drives (VFDs) that adjust speed to match inflow, or multi-pump lag staging, require a different analysis.
  • Allowable starts per hour depend on the specific motor and starter type (for example, soft starters or VFDs reduce starting heat compared to across-the-line starts). Check the motor manufacturer's data sheet for the governing limit.
  • Testing a station with a throttled clean-water supply near half of pump capacity produces the fastest cycling condition.
  • Actual float or transducer setpoints can differ from design drawings, which changes the effective volume V. Confirm setpoints against the approved control narrative (the document specifying pump elevations and sequence) and check wet well geometry against Hydraulic Institute intake guidance [2].

The calculator runs in the browser and stores your last entered values in browser storage (localStorage) so they stay filled in between visits.
