An Offline Chlorine Dosing Calculator for Water Main and Tank Disinfection

May 2026

Disinfecting a new water main or storage tank requires converting a target chlorine concentration, given in milligrams per liter (mg/L, equivalent to parts per million in water), into a practical quantity of liquid bleach (sodium hypochlorite) or dry chlorine (calcium hypochlorite). The Chlorine Dosing and Disinfection Calculator handles this conversion across four tabs: Pipeline for water mains under American Water Works Association (AWWA) standard C651 [1], Tank / Structure for storage tanks under AWWA C652 [2], Batch Solution for mixing spray or swab solutions, and Dechlorination for sizing neutralizing chemicals before flushing water is discharged [1][3].

To use the tool, select a tab, choose the disinfection method, and enter the pipe dimensions or tank volume, target dose, and chemical strength. For pipelines, the calculator computes internal water volume in gallons (gal) from cylinder geometry: V = (π/4) × (d/12)² × L × 7.4805, where d is the pipe inside diameter in inches (in), L is segment length in feet (ft), and 7.4805 converts cubic feet to gallons (equivalent to the common approximation V ≈ 0.0408 × d² × L). It then applies the standard pounds formula to find required pounds of available chlorine (Cl₂): lb Cl₂ = V_MG × dose × 8.34, where V_MG is water volume in million gallons (MG, equal to V / 1,000,000), dose is target concentration in mg/L, and 8.34 is the weight of water in pounds per gallon (lb/gal).

From the required chlorine mass, product quantity depends on the chemical source and its percent available chlorine (S). For liquid sodium hypochlorite, gal = lb Cl₂ / (8.34 × S / 100), where trade percent approximates pounds of available chlorine per gallon divided by 8.34 (for example, 12.5% strength supplies about 1.04 lb Cl₂ per gallon). For granular calcium hypochlorite, lb product = lb Cl₂ / (S / 100), typically at 65% strength. For the C651 tablet method [1], the calculator counts standard 5-gram tablets at 65% strength (3.25 grams of available chlorine each), rounded up. On the pipeline tab, default doses match AWWA C651 continuous feed (25 mg/L), slug (100 mg/L concentrated plug moved through the pipe), and tablet (25 mg/L) methods [1]. On the tank tab, defaults match AWWA C652 Method 1 (full tank at 10 mg/L) and Method 3 (bottom 5% of tank volume at 50 mg/L), while Method 2 spray solutions (200 mg/L) can be mixed on the Batch Solution tab [2].

The page runs offline as a single HTML file with no external libraries, and entered values are saved on your device using browser localStorage (local storage inside your web browser). Use the clear button to reset saved inputs.

Assumptions and limits

The dechlorination tab uses the AWWA C651 neutralization table values (pounds of chemical per mg/L of chlorine residual per 100,000 gal of water) [1][3]:

Sulfur dioxide          0.8 lb
Sodium bisulfite        1.2 lb
Sodium sulfite          1.4 lb
Sodium thiosulfate      1.2 lb
Ascorbic acid           2.1 lb
  • Dechlorination rates are stoichiometric (the exact theoretical chemical ratio). Because reaction rates vary (sodium thiosulfate reacts slowly at low pH), crews typically add modest excess and confirm zero measurable chlorine residual with a test kit before discharging flush water [3].
  • Liquid sodium hypochlorite loses strength during storage and in warm weather. If tested strength is below the labeled trade percentage, enter the actual strength in the calculator.
  • Project specifications, discharge permits, and the referenced AWWA standards govern contact times, end-of-hold residuals, flushing, and bacteriological water-quality testing [1][2].
