Pipe Flushing and Scour Velocity Calculator

August 2026

Flushing a water main (a pressurized potable water distribution pipe) relies on water velocity to move trapped air, loose sediment, and biofilm (surface microbial growth) out of the pipe. This calculator converts between flow rate and full-pipe velocity, estimates pipe storage volume, and calculates the flush duration and total water volume needed to exchange a chosen number of pipe volumes (multiples of the water held inside the flushed segment).

Enter the pipe's actual inside diameter D (in inches, rather than nominal pipe size, since wall thickness varies by material and pressure class), the length flushed L (ft), and the number of pipe volumes N. Choose either to solve for the required flow from a target velocity or to solve for the velocity produced by an available flow (for example, from a hydrant flow test). From full-pipe continuity (Q = A · V with circular area A = (π / 4) · (D / 12)² and 448.831 gpm per cfs), the tool uses V = 0.4085 · Q / D² and Q = 2.448 · V · D², where V is average velocity in feet per second (ft/s), Q is flow rate in gallons per minute (gpm), and D is inside diameter in inches. Pipe capacity per foot is vft = 0.0408 · D² (gal/ft, from (π / 4) · 7.4805 / 144), total pipe volume is Vol = vft · L (gal), total water used is Voltotal = N · Vol (gal), and flush duration is t = Voltotal / Q (min).

Because velocity varies inversely with D², doubling pipe diameter cuts velocity to one quarter at the same flow rate. Reaching a 5.0 ft/s unidirectional scour (pipe-wall cleaning) velocity requires about 783 gpm in an 8 in. main, but about 3,133 gpm in a 16 in. main [3]. A single 2-1/2-inch fire hydrant outlet typically passes around 1,000 to 1,200 gpm while maintaining usable residual pressure (pressure remaining in the main during flow) [2]. Unidirectional flushing addresses this by closing isolating valves in a planned sequence so available flow moves in one direction through a single pipe run rather than splitting across looped mains [3].

Notes and limits
  • AWWA C651 minimum velocity: The calculator's gauge displays bands below 2.5 ft/s, 2.5 to 5.0 ft/s, and 5.0+ ft/s, with presets at 2.5 ft/s and 5.0 ft/s plus a custom field. Note that AWWA (American Water Works Association) C651-14 and later editions set the minimum flushing velocity for disinfecting new water mains at 3.0 ft/s (increased from 2.5 ft/s in the 2005 edition) [1]. Enter 3.0 in the custom target field when checking compliance with current C651.
  • Disinfection and dechlorination: New-main disinfection involves flushing before chlorination and flushing out heavily chlorinated water afterward [1]. Heavily chlorinated water generally requires dechlorination (chemical neutralization) before discharging to storm drains or surface waters. For dosing calculations, see the chlorine dosing and disinfection calculator.
  • Valve and hydrant operation: Hydrants and isolation valves are normally opened and closed slowly to limit pressure surges and sudden sediment disturbance [2], [3].
  • Available hydrant flow: When required flow approaches hydrant capacity, confirm available discharge and residual pressure with a hydrant flow test (measuring outlet velocity pressure and system pressure) [2].
  • Local storage: Inputs are stored locally in the browser via localStorage and can be cleared with the reset button.
