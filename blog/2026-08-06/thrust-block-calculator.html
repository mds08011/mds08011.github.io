Thrust Block Sizing Calculator

August 2026

When a pressurized water main (a primary distribution pipe) changes direction, branches, ends at a cap, or changes size, internal water pressure creates an unbalanced outward force at the fitting. Common fittings that carry this thrust include bends, tees (T-shaped branch connections), dead ends, and reducers (fittings that step down to a smaller pipe diameter). That force has to be resisted either by a concrete thrust block pushing against the undisturbed trench wall or by restrained joints (mechanical locking joints that transfer the load into friction along the buried pipe). This calculator computes the unbalanced thrust force and the required concrete bearing area for horizontal thrust blocks [1][2].

The calculator uses the pipe's cross-sectional area based on its outside diameter (OD), A = (π / 4) × OD², where OD is in inches (in) and A is in square inches (in²). Thrust design uses OD rather than the inside pipe bore because internal pressure acts out to the gasket seal at the joint, which is close to the outside diameter [1]. From the hydrostatic test pressure P in pounds per square inch (psi), the unbalanced thrust force T in pounds (lb) is T = 2 × P × A × sin(θ / 2) for a bend of deflection angle θ (in degrees), T = P × A for a dead end, cap, or tee branch, and T = P × (A1 − A2) for a reducer between larger area A1 and smaller area A2 (both in in²). The required block bearing area Ab in square feet (ft²) is Ab = (T × SF) / Sb, where SF is the dimensionless safety factor (default 1.5) and Sb is the soil's safe horizontal bearing value in pounds per square foot (psf) [2][3]. If you enter an optional vertical height H (ft), the tool solves the required width W = Ab / H (ft). Leaving height blank sizes a square face and draws the block to scale.

The required bearing area depends directly on the soil's safe bearing value Sb. The soil presets in the tool are general textbook values for undisturbed native soil along the trench wall, not loose backfill placed back into the trench after excavation. In soft clay, saturated ground, organic soils such as muck or peat, or disturbed fill, safe bearing capacity drops sharply and the required block face becomes impractically large. Selecting the muck/peat preset (0 psf) in the calculator stops reporting a block area and recommends restrained joints instead, which carry the thrust along a calculated restrained pipe length rather than relying on the trench wall [1][3].

Assumptions and limits
  • Size blocks for the hydrostatic test pressure (the elevated water pressure applied during leakage testing), not the lower normal working pressure, because the fitting experiences its peak load during testing.
  • Thrust at a bend acts outward along the bisector of the bend (the line splitting the bend angle in half). Concrete blocks must be poured on the outside of the bend against undisturbed trench wall, never against backfill.
  • Keep concrete off the pipe joints, gaskets, and bolts by wrapping the joint (for example, with polyethylene sheeting) and bearing the block against the fitting body so the joint stays accessible for repair.
  • This calculator covers horizontal bearing blocks only. Vertical bends that push upward require a concrete gravity (weight) block sized to resist uplift or restrained joints [2].
  • Confirm site-specific soil bearing values with a geotechnical engineer, especially when required bearing areas are large or trench conditions are uncertain.

The calculator runs in the web browser with no external scripts and stores your inputs locally in browser storage (localStorage).
