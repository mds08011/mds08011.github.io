Manning's Open-Channel Flow and Normal Depth Calculator

July 2026

This calculator applies Manning's equation to steady, uniform gravity flow in partially full circular pipes, rectangular channels, and trapezoidal channels [1]. In forward mode, it calculates discharge and velocity from a given water depth. In reverse mode, it solves for normal depth (the steady uniform flow depth on a constant slope) from a target design flow.

In US customary units, Manning's equation is Q = (1.486 / n) · A · R^(2/3) · S^(1/2) with mean velocity V = Q / A, where Q is flow rate in cubic feet per second (cfs), n is Manning's roughness coefficient (dimensionless), A is cross-sectional flow area in square feet (ft²), R = A / P is hydraulic radius in feet (ft), P is wetted perimeter (the boundary length in contact with water) in feet (ft), S is channel slope in ft/ft, and V is average velocity in ft/s [1]. Results are also reported in gallons per minute (GPM) and million gallons per day (MGD). For a circular pipe of inside diameter D (ft) flowing at depth d (ft), the central angle in radians is θ = 2 · acos(1 − 2d / D) (with acos returning radians), giving A = (D² / 8)(θ − sinθ), P = D · θ / 2, and water-surface top width T = D · sin(θ / 2) [1].

Because Manning's equation has no closed-form algebraic solution for depth d in partially full pipes or trapezoidal channels, reverse mode uses bisection (repeatedly evaluating Q(d) at the midpoint of a bracket and halving the interval over 60 iterations). In a circular pipe, gravity discharge peaks near d/D = 0.938 and then decreases slightly toward full flow because wetted perimeter grows faster than flow area near the top of the pipe [1]. The solver searches only up to d/D = 0.938, where Q(d) increases monotonically (strictly upward with depth). If the entered flow exceeds that gravity peak, the calculator reports that the pipe will surcharge (fill completely and operate under pressure). The calculator also computes the dimensionless Froude number Fr = V / √(g · A / T), where g = 32.174 ft/s² is gravitational acceleration and A / T is hydraulic depth (ft), to classify the flow regime as subcritical (Fr < 1, slower tranquil flow) or supercritical (Fr > 1, rapid flow) [3]. Between roughly Fr = 0.9 and 1.1, the water surface becomes wavy and unstable, so the tool flags readings in that range [3].

Assumptions and limits
  • Manning's equation assumes steady, uniform flow on a constant slope, away from drops, bends, gates, junctions, or downstream backwater.
  • Sanitary sewer design standards such as the Ten-State Standards generally require a minimum self-cleansing velocity (velocity sufficient to limit solids deposition) of at least 2.0 ft/s at design flow, and often mandate a design n value (commonly 0.013) regardless of pipe material [2]. The calculator flags velocities below 2.0 ft/s.
  • Flow depth is referenced from the pipe invert (the lowest interior point of the pipe). Accumulated grit, sediment, or grease reduces the effective flow section compared with the nominal diameter.
  • Use the constructed invert slope between structures rather than ground surface slope.
  • Estimating flow from a single depth reading with Manning's equation is approximate (often within 10% to 20% at best). It is useful for rough checks such as screening for inflow and infiltration (I&I, stormwater or groundwater entering a sanitary sewer), rather than billing or formal flow metering.

The calculator runs in the browser and saves inputs in browser local storage (localStorage, a built-in browser store on the current device) until cleared.
