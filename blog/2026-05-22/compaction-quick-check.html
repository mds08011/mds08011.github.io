Checking Field Compaction Against the Proctor

May 2026

Overview

A nuclear density gauge measures the in-place wet density and moisture content of compacted soil [2]. Project specifications generally require a minimum relative compaction (percent compaction), defined as the in-place dry density divided by the laboratory Proctor maximum dry density from a Standard Proctor (ASTM D698) or Modified Proctor (ASTM D1557) test [1]. This calculator converts a wet or dry gauge reading into percent compaction, compares field moisture against the laboratory optimum moisture content, and computes the wet density needed to pass at the current moisture content.

Formulas

From a wet density reading and moisture content, dry density and relative compaction are:

γ_dry = γ_wet / (1 + w / 100)
RC = (γ_dry / γ_dry,max) × 100

Solving in reverse gives the required wet density at the current moisture content:

γ_wet,req = (RC_req / 100) × γ_dry,max × (1 + w / 100)

Here γ_dry is field dry density in pounds per cubic foot (pcf), γ_wet is field wet density in pcf, w is moisture content in percent, RC is relative compaction in percent, γ_dry,max is the laboratory Proctor maximum dry density in pcf, RC_req is the specified minimum compaction in percent, and γ_wet,req is the required wet density in pcf. The tool also accepts dry density directly if the gauge displays it.

Moisture content is reported alongside density because a compacted soil layer (lift) can pass the density requirement and still perform poorly if its moisture is far from optimum. Clay compacted wet of optimum (above the laboratory optimum moisture content) can flex or pump under later lifts or inside a pipe zone (the backfill around a buried pipe). Clay compacted dry of optimum can reach high density in a stiff, high-suction state that later settles or collapses if it becomes wet under a slab or structure. Showing the moisture offset from optimum makes it straightforward to check both density and moisture against the project specification.

Assumptions and limits
  • This calculator checks arithmetic only. It assumes the correct laboratory Proctor curve has been selected for the soil being tested, the gauge is properly calibrated, and any required oversize correction has already been applied. The testing agency's report is the official record.
  • Relative compaction above 100% is physically possible when field compaction energy exceeds the laboratory effort, but values above roughly 102% to 103% warrant checking for a mismatched Proctor curve, a mix-up between Standard (ASTM D698) and Modified (ASTM D1557) Proctor tests where Modified maximum densities run several pcf higher [1], or oversize gravel and cobbles raising the gauge reading.
  • When more than 5% of the soil by weight is retained on the oversize sieve (typically the 3/4-inch or No. 4 sieve depending on the Proctor method), an oversize particle correction under ASTM D4718 applies [3].

The calculator runs offline in a single HTML file using plain JavaScript with no external libraries, and saves your inputs in your browser's local storage (localStorage).
