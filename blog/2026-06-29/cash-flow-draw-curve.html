Cash Flow Draw Curve Calculator

June 2026

This calculator compares a construction project's monthly billing schedule against the cash actually received after retention (also called retainage, a percentage held back from each progress payment until closeout) and payment lag (the delay, in months, between submitting a monthly pay application and receiving payment) [1][2]. The difference between cumulative billed revenue and cumulative cash received is the financing gap, which shows earned revenue that has not yet been collected.

Monthly billing can be entered directly or generated from a cumulative fraction curve f(t), where t = m / T is the elapsed fraction of the project duration (m is the month number from 1 to T, and T is total project duration in months). Four curve shapes are available: a smoothstep S-curve (a cumulative curve with a slow start, steeper middle, and slow finish) given by f(t) = 3t² − 2t³, a front-loaded curve (higher billing early) given by f(t) = 2t − t², a back-loaded curve (higher billing late) given by f(t) = t², and a linear curve (equal monthly amounts) given by f(t) = t. For each month m, projected billing is B_m = [f(m/T) − f((m − 1)/T)] · V, where B_m is monthly billing in dollars and V is total contract value in dollars.

Each monthly progress payment received is R = (1 − r) · B_m (in dollars), where r is the retention fraction (for example, 0.05 for 5%). That net payment is recorded at month m + L_p, where L_p is the payment lag in months. Total accumulated retention, r · V (in dollars), is released as a lump sum at month T + N, where N is the release delay in months after the final billing month. The peak financing gap is the maximum value of cumulative billed − cumulative received (in dollars) across the timeline, which indicates the working-capital carrying cost (the cost of financing uncollected receivables) over the job.

Assumptions and limits
  • This models revenue timing only. Actual project cash position also depends on outbound cost timing, such as weekly labor payroll, supplier invoices (often net 30, meaning due in 30 days), and subcontractor payments (often pay-when-paid, meaning paid after the general contractor collects from the owner). Retention held on subcontractors can partially offset retention held by the owner.
  • Progress billing relies on a schedule of values (SOV), which breaks the contract sum into line items reviewed by the owner's representative before payment [1][2]. Mobilization line items cover initial project setup costs, while overstating early activities that have not been completed is typically rejected during pay-application review.
  • Where a contract permits billing for materials stored on site before installation, owners typically require invoices, proof of title, and insurance documentation [1][2].
  • The governing contract defines actual payment dates, retention percentages, and release conditions. Depending on public or private contract terms, retention may be reduced in stages or released after substantial completion (when the work is usable by the owner) and completion of the punch list (final minor corrections) [1][2].

The calculator runs in the browser and saves inputs in browser local storage (localStorage, a built-in browser store on the current device) until cleared.
