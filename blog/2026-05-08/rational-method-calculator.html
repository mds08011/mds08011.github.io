Rational Method Peak Flow Calculator

May 2026

The Rational Method estimates peak stormwater runoff from a small drainage area (watershed) using Q = C × i × A, where Q is peak discharge in cubic feet per second (cfs), C is a dimensionless runoff coefficient representing the fraction of rainfall that becomes runoff (0 to 1), i is average rainfall intensity in inches per hour (in/hr), and A is drainage area in acres (ac). One acre-inch per hour equals 1.008 cfs, which is conventionally taken as 1.0. The Rational Method Peak Flow Calculator combines a sub-area runoff coefficient table with a segmented travel-time calculator to evaluate Q.

To compute a composite runoff coefficient for mixed surfaces, add sub-area rows (roof, pavement, gravel, lawn, or custom) and enter each area in acres. The tool computes the area-weighted average C = Σ(C_k × A_k) / ΣA_k, where C_k and A_k are the runoff coefficient and area (ac) of each sub-area. Next, build the time of concentration (Tc, the travel time in minutes from the hydraulically most distant point in the watershed to the outlet) by adding flow-path segments following Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) Technical Release 55 (TR-55) and National Engineering Handbook (NEH) Part 630 Chapter 15 methods [1]:

  • Sheet flow (shallow overland flow at the start of the path, limited to 100 ft under current NRCS NEH Part 630 Chapter 15 guidance, reduced from 300 ft in the original 1986 TR-55 [1]): Tt = 0.007 × (n × L)^0.8 / (P₂^0.5 × s^0.4), where Tt is travel time in hours (converted to minutes in the tool), n is the dimensionless sheet-flow roughness coefficient, L is flow length in feet (ft), s is land slope in ft/ft, and P₂ is the 2-year 24-hour rainfall depth in inches (in), looked up by site location on the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) Atlas 14 Precipitation Frequency Data Server [2].
  • Shallow concentrated flow: average velocity is V = 20.328 × √s (ft/s) on paved surfaces or V = 16.135 × √s (ft/s) on unpaved surfaces, where s is slope in ft/ft, and travel time is Tt = L / (3600 × V) in hours (with L in ft and 3600 converting seconds to hours) [1].
  • Channel or pipe flow: travel time is Tt = L / (3600 × V) in hours, where L is segment length in ft and V is average velocity in ft/s (which can be calculated separately using the Manning's flow calculator).

The Rational Method assumes peak flow occurs when storm duration equals Tc, so rainfall intensity i is read at Tc for the required storm return period. In the Intensity section, either enter i (in/hr) directly from a local Intensity-Duration-Frequency (IDF) curve or NOAA Atlas 14 intensity table [2], or select the power-law IDF equation i = a / (Tc + b)^n, where Tc is in minutes and a, b, and n are curve-fit constants from the local drainage manual (this exponent n is distinct from the sheet-flow roughness coefficient). All calculations run offline in the browser, and inputs are saved on your device in browser localStorage.

Assumptions and limits
  • Watershed area limit: the Rational Method applies to small watersheds without significant ponding or detention. Most drainage manuals cap applicability around 100 to 200 acres (the calculator warns above 100 acres and 200 acres, and if composite C exceeds 0.95). Larger basins require a hydrograph method.
  • Minimum Tc floor: very short travel times on steep or fully paved sites yield very high rainfall intensities, so local criteria typically impose a minimum Tc floor (commonly 5 to 10 minutes, with a default of 5 minutes in the calculator).
  • Flow path selection: Tc should represent the hydraulically longest path (longest travel time to the outlet), which is not always the geographically longest path.
  • Local criteria and comparisons: local city or county hydrology manuals govern runoff coefficients, Tc procedures (including modified Rational procedures used by some jurisdictions), and IDF curves. When comparing pre-development and post-development runoff, use the same method, return period, and IDF source for both cases.
