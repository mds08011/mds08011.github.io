SWPPP Rain Event and Inspection Tracker

July 2026

This browser-based tracker logs rain events and site inspections for a construction Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP). Under the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Construction General Permit (CGP) or a state equivalent, sites must conduct inspections on a routine schedule and after qualifying rain events [1]. You enter three rules from the governing permit (the qualifying storm threshold in inches, the post-rain inspection due window in calendar days, and the routine inspection interval in calendar days), then log 24-hour rainfall totals and completed inspections (routine, post-rain, or pre-storm). The tool reports which qualifying storms still need an inspection and when the next routine inspection is due.

The status summary uses three date-based checks. A rain event with 24-hour rainfall R (in) qualifies when R >= R_thresh, where R_thresh (in) is the permit threshold (commonly 0.25 in under the 2022 EPA CGP) [1]. A qualifying storm on date d_rain is marked covered if any logged inspection falls on date d_insp within d_rain <= d_insp <= d_rain + W, where W is the post-rain due window (calendar days). Otherwise the storm shows as due, or overdue once the current date passes d_rain + W. The next routine inspection due date is d_next = d_last + I, where d_last is the date of the most recent logged inspection and I is the routine interval (calendar days, commonly 7 or 14 days) [1].

In JavaScript, calling new Date("2026-07-07") parses an ISO date string as midnight Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). In US time zones (west of Greenwich), UTC midnight falls on the previous local calendar day, which would shift logged dates by one day. To keep calculations in local calendar time, both this tracker and the working-day tracker split the string and construct local dates from numeric year, month, and day components, where unary + converts each text part to a number and - 1 accounts for JavaScript's 0-to-11 month index (0 for January through 11 for December):

var p = "2026-07-07".split("-");
var d = new Date(+p[0], +p[1] - 1, +p[2]);
Notes and limits
  • Non-qualifying rain: Recording rainfall days below the qualifying threshold documents ongoing gauge checks alongside signed inspection forms, which helps avoid record gaps that can lead to a notice of violation (a formal regulatory citation) during a permit audit.
  • Forecast-based requirements: Some state permits require actions before a forecasted storm, such as pre-storm inspections or a Rain Event Action Plan (REAP, a pre-storm checklist used in states such as California). Track those triggers from weather forecasts, and use the pre-storm inspection type to record completed checks.
  • Permit-specific rules: This tool uses simple calendar-day windows. If the federal, state, or site-specific SWPPP counts working days, combines consecutive storm days, or allows reduced frequency on stabilized areas, follow the permit and SWPPP terms.
  • Local browser storage: The tracker is a single HTML page written in plain JavaScript that runs offline and stores all entries only in browser localStorage (local storage on your device). Clearing browser site data or switching devices removes the log, so maintain signed inspection forms and the project SWPPP as the official compliance records.
