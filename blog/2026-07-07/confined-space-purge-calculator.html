Confined Space Ventilation Purge Time Calculator

July 2026

This calculator estimates initial forced-air ventilation purge time for enclosed utility structures such as manholes, wet wells (underground wastewater collection chambers), vaults, and channels. It is a scheduling aid for estimating when to take pre-entry air readings and does not clear a confined space for entry.

The calculator computes space volume for a circular section (V = (π / 4) × D^2 × H), a rectangular section (V = L × W × H), or a direct volume entry, then estimates effective airflow and initial purge time:

Q_eff = Q_rated × (E / 100),   t = (V × N) / Q_eff

Here D, L, W, and H are inside diameter, length, width, and depth or height in feet (ft), V is space volume in cubic feet (cf), Q_rated is the blower's rated free-air output in cubic feet per minute (CFM), E is the estimated delivery efficiency in percent after duct friction losses, Q_eff is effective delivered airflow in CFM, N is the target number of complete air changes (dimensionless, commonly 7 to 10 in utility confined-space programs, though the employer's written program governs), and t is the estimated purge time in minutes (min).

Flexible ventilation duct length and bends add static pressure that substantially reduces axial blower delivery. The tool provides rough derating presets (85%, 65%, and 50%) along with a custom percentage field so you can enter actual delivered airflow from the blower manufacturer's duct-loss data.

Entry clearance under Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) permit-required confined space standards (29 CFR 1910.146 for general industry [1] and 29 CFR 1926 Subpart AA for construction [2]) requires calibrated atmospheric testing by a qualified or competent person under a written entry permit, along with continuous ventilation and continuous monitoring during occupancy. Standard four-gas monitoring checks oxygen (O₂), lower explosive limit (LEL, the lowest flammable gas concentration that can ignite), hydrogen sulfide (H₂S, a toxic, heavier-than-air sewer gas), and carbon monoxide (CO, a toxic gas from engine exhaust). Because gases stratify by density, testing is required at the top, middle, and bottom of vertical spaces [1][2].

Assumptions and limits
  • Supply ducting is typically extended near the bottom of a vertical space (or far end of a horizontal space) so fresh air displaces heavier gases such as H₂S from the bottom up rather than mixing only the upper zone.
  • Place the blower intake upwind and clear of generator exhaust, vehicle exhaust, and the space's own vent discharge to avoid drawing CO or vented gases into the supply stream.
  • Sewer-connected structures can re-contaminate quickly from upstream flow surges (slugs) or sediment off-gassing. If atmospheric tests still fail after a full calculated purge, the space likely has an active contaminant source requiring re-evaluation by the entry supervisor rather than additional purge time alone.
  • OSHA rules [1][2] and the employer's written confined-space program govern all entry procedures, including the entry permit, outside attendant (who monitors entrants and summons rescue), non-entry retrieval equipment, and required air changes.
  • The calculator is a single offline HTML file with no external dependencies and stores inputs in browser localStorage (local storage on your device) until cleared.
