Scaling Pump Performance with the Affinity Laws

June 2026

This calculator applies the centrifugal pump affinity laws to estimate how flow, head (pressure expressed as feet of water column), and shaft power change when a pump runs at a different speed or with a trimmed impeller (the rotating wheel inside the pump machined to a smaller outside diameter). It accepts speed as Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) electrical frequency in hertz (Hz, where full U.S. utility frequency is 60 Hz), as shaft speed in revolutions per minute (RPM), or as impeller diameter in inches.

At a fixed impeller diameter, let r = N₂ / N₁ be the ratio of the new operating speed N₂ to the rated speed N₁ (both in Hz or both in RPM). The affinity equations scale rated flow Q₁ (gallons per minute, gpm), rated head H₁ (feet, ft), and optional rated power P₁ (brake horsepower, BHP) to the new condition [1][2]:

Q₂ = Q₁·r    H₂ = H₁·r²    P₂ = P₁·r³

For an AC induction motor, shaft speed follows the electrical supply frequency closely apart from a small lag called slip, so the ratio of two VFD frequencies in Hz is nearly the same as the ratio of shaft speeds in RPM. The same equations provide an approximate scaling for impeller trim at constant speed using r = D₂ / D₁, where D₁ is the full diameter and D₂ is the trimmed diameter (inches), though accuracy drops when the trim exceeds roughly 10% to 15% [2]. Alongside the single scaled point, the calculator prints a speed table from 50% to 100% of rated speed or diameter.

Assumptions and limits
  • The affinity laws shift the pump's own performance curve, not necessarily the operating point in a piping system. The actual operating point is where the shifted pump curve intersects the system head curve. If static head (vertical elevation lift) makes up a large share of rated head, flow drops faster than r as speed decreases, and flow stops once pump head falls below static head [1].
  • Scaling a single point is an estimate. Use the manufacturer's published family of speed curves or trim curves when available [2].
  • Running at low speed slows shaft-mounted motor cooling fans and can drop pipe velocity below solids-carrying minimums in wastewater service, so manufacturers often specify a minimum speed limit (commonly 40% to 60% of rated speed) [1].
  • When checking pump performance against scaled values, verify actual output frequency on the drive keypad (not just the commanded speed setpoint). Motor current draw well above the r³ power scaling can point to mechanical drag or a fluid thicker than water. Low output at full 60 Hz speed can indicate a clogged impeller, worn wear rings (internal sealing rings inside the casing), or reversed three-phase motor rotation (which should be checked and corrected by a qualified electrician).

The calculator runs in the browser and stores your last inputs locally in browser storage (localStorage).
